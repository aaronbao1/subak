The smallest useful common mechanism is a finite joint path law with source/procedure identity and an independently labelled stress suite. Sample the same historical block/shared shock for all affected entities and dates, price every path under the effective tariff, then aggregate before computing risk. Preserve failed or exhausted high-workload paths as numerical/simulation failures; dropping them selectively would bias risk downward. Correlated observation loss requires coverage and maturity state, not simply wider residuals: a block missing its most expensive requests is not a valid realized-spend label.

`JointPaths` already freezes nested inputs and preserves path indices across entities and dates (`uncertainty.py:124–170`); existing tests exercise dependence, signed refunds, and cumulative horizon quantiles. `cumulative_interval` selects mature, exact-horizon, fixed-procedure residuals and rejects overlapping periods (`:68–107`). The service correctly advertises no probability/joint-path capability (`service.py:228–229`). These are useful foundations. There is no fitted enterprise tail model, expected-shortfall function, operational workload bound, or release-frequency estimator. Do not imply otherwise when promoting new functionality.

## Four immediate changes

1. **Fail explicitly on derived overflow.** Finite input validation does not imply finite arithmetic. At present `expected_excess` subtracts then weights without a finite-result check; cumulative residual and endpoint calculations likewise escape checks. Validate residuals, totals, endpoint arithmetic, weighted terms and reductions. Prefer an explicit numeric failure over saturation. Known recorded Decimal amounts should remain accessible when a probabilistic calculation fails. Avoid returning the string/JSON representation of infinity under an `available` status.

2. **Add atom-safe expected shortfall with explicit semantics.** For a declared weighted finite law, use `ES(alpha) = q + sum(w * max(total-q,0))/(1-alpha)` with its weighted left-inverse quantile `q`, finite alpha in `[0,1)`, and validated arithmetic. Keep `expected_excess(budget)` as a different functional: it is expected overspend above a selected budget, not expected shortfall at a confidence level. Negative net-cost paths remain permissible when the target includes refunds; do not silently clip them. The source paper establishes why a strict conditional average above VaR differs at atoms, and supports finite scenario calculations: [Rockafellar–Uryasev, Conditional Value-at-Risk for General Loss Distributions](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~rtr/papers/rtr187-CVaR2.pdf), especially its discussion on PDF pages 2–3.

3. **Keep law provenance separate from calibration claims.** Each risk summary needs law kind, weight source, exact target/unit, procedure/model/policy identity, independent mature block count, horizon and limitations. Repeating blocks or increasing Monte Carlo count does not create additional historical extremes. Bootstrap zero exceedance means zero under that empirical support. It does not establish zero future risk. Expose stress costs/severity independently when stress frequencies are unidentified; do not append arbitrary catastrophe weights to a fitted law and call its quantile calibrated.

4. **Run adversarial compound tests and report failures.** Prioritize release-wide effort change; outage plus expensive fallback and cache loss; peak-dependent telemetry dropout; backlog crossing the reporting boundary; and a single economically dominant burst. Retries can amplify overload and a timeout can leave remote side effects intact; this mechanism is supported by [Amazon's timeouts/retries guidance](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter/). Actual AI billing consequences remain a conditional inference requiring receipts. Require conserved billed totals, unchanged as-of evidence, visible incomplete states, and worst-window errors in addition to pooled averages.

## Conditions for a future generative extension

A finite empirical law has finite moments when every input and derived output is finite. That does not establish finite moments for an underlying fitted parametric process. The prior independent risk review correctly requires `E[exp(eta)] < infinity` for a published mean intensity across the **full** joint predictive law, including uncertain loadings, interaction terms and posterior mixtures. Ordinary finite polynomial moments are insufficient. For independent standard normals `B,H`, integrating `exp(BH)` conditional on `H` yields `exp(H²/2)`, whose expectation diverges. This counterexample is mathematical, not an empirical claim about AI spend.

Finite horizon also fails to establish nonexplosion when recursive actions may occur with no positive elapsed time. A conditional simulator needs an event/work bound or a justified nonexplosive process. An exhausted simulation must not masquerade as completed cheap work. Shared reservations/caps require chronological global state; one entity's accepted work can reduce another's feasible work. An internal allocation is only a comparison threshold. Provider-enforced or simulation-enforced bounds require their own scope, pre-start liability bounds and settlement assumptions. No external kill switch is authorized by the product scope.
