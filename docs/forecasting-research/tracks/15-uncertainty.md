# 15 — Predictive distributions, cumulative calibration, and honest risk

Fresh research, accessed 2026-10-24. This track addresses the master plan's supported-uncertainty/unavailable handoff, the frontend's requirement to avoid presenting overspending as inevitable, and Factory's separation of documentary evidence from numerical forecasts. The original discussion motivates comparing weekly and monthly planning periods; it does not establish a probability model. No enterprise predictive experiment was run.

## Decision and evidence status

**Established evidence:** predictive intervals require assumptions even when called distribution-free; marginal coverage differs from conditional coverage; aggregation requires dependence information; delayed outcomes affect online calibration. **Inference:** AI spending can combine intermittency, skewed severity, correlated launches, provider changes and late charges, so an apparently reasonable daily interval can seriously misstate company-period risk. **Proposed design:** compare a direct cumulative-target model against coherent joint sample paths, calibrate the exact decision target, and permit point-only, scenario-only and unavailable outputs. **Unvalidated hypotheses:** block residual paths plus a modest pooled predictor may be competitive; adaptive calibration may help after shifts; hurdle distributions may improve sparse teams. None is a universal recommendation.

Define a target before choosing an uncertainty method. For period P, entity g and forecast information vintage v:

`total(g,P | v) = recorded_to_date(g,P,v) + unreported_or_revised_incurred(g,P | v) + future_continuation(g,P | v) + remaining_fixed_charges(g,P | v)`.

Every component needs a nonoverlapping coverage rule. A reported charge and a telemetry estimate for the same incurred activity are alternative evidence, not independent additive costs. Known fixed charges may be deterministic; uncertain seats, contract terms, foreign exchange, tier triggers or refunds need separate uncertainty. For net charges, revisions can be negative. A nonnegative gross-usage model must not silently become a net-invoice model. Forecasts conditional on a proposed price or project schedule should retain that condition.

## Distribution alternatives worth comparing

| Candidate | Useful conditions/data | Main tradeoff and failure | Overturning experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parametric state-space/regression distribution | Moderate history, stable residual mechanism; explicit support and changing scale | Cheap and inspectable; misspecified tail, zero mass or shared shock can invalidate risk | Compare period coverage, upper-tail loss and negative-support violations against empirical paths |
| IID residual simulation | Genuine out-of-sample errors approximately stable with little remaining dependence | Flexible shape, low cost; destroys clusters and cannot invent unseen residual extremes | Preserve everything except resampling dependence; compare cumulative coverage |
| Moving/stationary block residual paths | Enough reasonably comparable history to identify burst length and aligned teams | Preserves local temporal dependence; sensitive to block length, breaks and scarce joint bursts | Sweep defensible lengths and include untouched regime-change periods |
| Direct quantile or distribution regression | Sufficient examples of the exact 7/14/30-day or remaining-month target; pooled features where justified | Handles skew/heteroscedasticity; crossing quantiles, sparse upper tails, no joint path by default | Compare horizon-specific pinball/interval scores and budget-event reliability |
| Joint parametric/copula/factor paths | Aligned histories, stable cross-team factors; sufficient joint events | Flexible dependence; covariance regularization and tail misspecification; substantially more operation/monitoring | Compare simultaneous shared-burst and company-total scores against independent marginals and blocks |
| Conformal calibration wrapper | Mature out-of-sample scores plus method-specific assumptions | Can correct intervals, cannot create missing historical support or a calibrated full distribution by naming it conformal | Compare uncalibrated, fixed, weighted and adaptive versions on identical held-out targets |

**Established evidence:** textbook residual simulation recursively generates possible futures; the simple residual-bootstrap illustration assumes uncorrelated, constant-variance errors and future errors resembling past errors. Its multi-step formulas are model-specific. This supports a baseline, not an assertion that fitted residuals automatically supply valid enterprise intervals. [Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, FPP3 §5.5](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html#distributional-forecasts-and-prediction-intervals)

The stationary bootstrap resamples random-length consecutive blocks and develops consistency for weakly dependent stationary observations. That is evidence for preserving local dependence when resampling, not a finite-sample predictive guarantee for an AI-spend regime change. **Proposed design:** resample aligned vectors across teams, using one block-index sequence for each joint path; independently resampling team blocks loses shared shocks. Estimate residuals at historical forecast origins, after fitting only data available then. [Politis and Romano, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870)

Distributional models must match support: count likelihoods describe counts, not arbitrary dollar amounts; positive continuous severity may use lognormal/Gamma components, and genuine zero mass may need a hurdle. Heavy-tailed signed errors do not automatically respect nonnegative gross spending. DeepAR demonstrates likelihood-based recursive sampling, including Gaussian/negative-binomial choices, and computes aggregate quantiles by summing each sampled path first. **Inference:** the mechanism is useful, but demand benchmarks cannot choose the right spend likelihood or establish enterprise calibration. [Salinas et al., 2020, §§4.1, 5.2.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2019.07.001)

Low-rank Gaussian copula processes combine flexible marginal distributions with time-varying cross-series covariance and reduce parameter burden. **Inference:** this offers a dependence challenger when many teams share shocks; it does not justify a Gaussian tail-dependence assumption. Tail stress comparisons must include common bursts, not only correlation averaged across ordinary days. [Salinas et al., NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/0b105cf1504c4e241fcc6d519ea962fb-Abstract.html)

## Mathematical distinctions that change the product

The following are exact illustrations, not forecasts or experiments.

1. **Mean, median and reserve are different quantities.** If a period costs 0 with probability 0.9 and 100 with probability 0.1, its mean is 10, median 0 and 95th percentile 100. A zero median does not imply zero expected spending. Expected totals add by linearity when means exist; medians and quantiles generally do not. Name the statistic in the API and interface. Under a specified linear underage/overage loss, the optimal reserve is the quantile `c_under/(c_under+c_over)`; choosing costs is a finance policy decision, not a statistical theorem about which percentile the business wants.
2. **Adding quantiles can understate a cumulative quantile.** Two independent days each cost 100 with probability 0.04, otherwise 0. Each daily 95th percentile is 0. The two-day total has probability `0.96² = 0.9216` at 0 and probability `0.9984` at or below 100; its 95th percentile is 100, not 0.
3. **Identical marginals can imply tenfold different budget risk.** Two teams each spend 100 with probability 0.1. If independent, `P(company total > 150)=0.01`; if their bursts always coincide, it is 0.10. Their standalone distributions are identical in both worlds. Summing marginal means still gives 20, while company 95th percentiles are 100 and 200 respectively.
4. **Temporal covariance drives cumulative width.** For finite-variance daily errors, `Var(sum_h e_h)=sum_h Var(e_h)+2 sum_(h<j) Cov(e_h,e_j)`. Seven equal-variance independent errors have total standard deviation `sqrt(7)σ`; seven perfectly shared errors have `7σ`. Cross-sectional reconciliation alone does not supply these cross-date covariances.

**Proposed design:** two architectures compete. Route A directly models each total target; calibrate residuals for that target, remaining-day count, scope and financial basis. Route B generates entity × date paths, preserves a path ID through pricing, allocation and aggregation, then computes period/company statistics. Route B can serve many rollups; Route A avoids estimating unnecessary daily dependence but can yield incoherent totals or horizon curves. Coherence and predictive calibration require separate checks. Enforce nondecreasing cumulative paths only for nonnegative gross quantities; refunds invalidate that constraint for net charges.

Marginal 90% intervals for seven daily values are not a 90% simultaneous band, and neither is automatically a calibrated interval for their sum. A union bound gives only 30% simultaneous coverage from seven 10% error bounds; independence would give `0.9^7 ≈ 47.8%`. A joint band using maximum standardized path error is a separate target and may be wide. Do not conflate simultaneous team coverage with each team's marginal coverage.

## What conformal claims actually mean

**Split/CQR.** With a predictor fitted independently of calibration responses and exchangeable calibration/test examples, split conformal uses a finite-sample corrected score rank. CQR learns lower/upper quantiles and calibrates `s=max(q_low−y, y−q_high)`, allowing width to vary with predictors. Its finite-sample statement is marginal; feature-dependent widths do not establish exact conditional coverage. **Proposed design:** treat CQR as an interval challenger using direct cumulative targets, not an automatic solution to overlapping time-series dependence. [Romano, Patterson and Candès, 2019](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/5103c3584b063c431bd1268e9b5e76fb-Abstract.html)

For n ordinary split-calibration scores and level `1−α`, the rank is `ceil((n+1)(1−α))`, with an infinity augmentation when that rank exceeds n. At 95%, n=18 gives rank 19 and no finite ordinary bound on an unrestricted response space; n=19 merely permits the largest observed score. This is a feasibility boundary, not evidence that 19 dependent windows suffice. Ties require careful rank semantics. The basic rank result is documented in the weighted-conformal paper. [Tibshirani et al., 2019, Lemma 1](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ryantibs/papers/weightedcp.pdf)

**EnbPI.** The main theorem assumes stationary strongly mixing errors, a Lipschitz error CDF and sufficiently accurate regression-error estimation. It bounds approximate marginal coverage error; the implemented interval does not add the theorem's worst-case correction. Nonstationary observations can still satisfy suitable residual assumptions, but arbitrary changing residual mechanisms do not. Its own change-point discussion describes degradation when old errors calibrate new behavior. Bootstrap ensemble fitting is distinct from joint future-path sampling. A point mass at zero can violate the stated Lipschitz-CDF assumption unless the residual construction removes it; do not cite this theorem unchanged for a hurdle target. [Xu and Xie, 2021, §4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/xu21h/xu21h.pdf)

**ACI.** Its update is `α_(t+1)=α_t+γ(α−err_t)`. Telescoping controls long-run empirical error frequency, with deviation bounded by `(max(α_1,1−α_1)+γ)/(Tγ)` under its extended-quantile conventions. This can involve infinite or empty prediction sets. It does not assert that today's conditional overspend chance equals α, nor that a short recent window is calibrated. Clipping α into [0,1], capping intervals at a pleasing amount, or dropping missed observations changes the algorithm and voids direct reuse of the proof. **Proposed design:** expose unbounded results honestly. [Gibbs and Candès, 2021, §4.1](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/file/0d441de75945e5acbc865406fc9a2559-Paper.pdf)

**Weighted and adaptive alternatives.** Covariate-shift weighting assumes unchanged `P(Y|X)` and known density ratios for its exact guarantee; estimated ratios add uncertainty. It is not equivalent to recency weighting. Nonexchangeable conformal instead bounds coverage loss using differences between original and swapped data distributions; drift robustness is qualified by those discrepancies, not free of them. Effective weighted sample size `(sum w)²/sum(w²)` is a useful diagnostic, not a theorem restoring exchangeability. [Tibshirani et al., 2019](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ryantibs/papers/weightedcp.pdf); [Barber et al., 2023](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-51/issue-2/Conformal-prediction-beyond-exchangeability/10.1214/23-AOS2276.pdf)

SPCI predicts future residual quantiles from residual history and establishes asymptotic conditional coverage under consistency conditions; it does not supply unrestricted exact finite-sample conditional coverage. Adaptive conformal work in 2024 tunes step sizes and gives local-interval regret control, improving the adaptation design but adding choices and monitoring. Compare these when residual dynamics justify their cost; do not translate regret or asymptotic results into a current-case guarantee. [Xu and Xie, 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/xu23r.html); [Gibbs and Candès, 2024](https://jmlr.org/papers/v25/22-1218.html)

The general limit is substantive: without distributional restrictions, exact conditional distribution-free coverage is incompatible with useful unrestricted intervals. Finance can receive subgroup diagnostics and explicit conditions, not a promise that a population marginal guarantee applies exactly to today's unusual research launch. [Barber et al., v2 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04684v2)

## Delayed, overlapping and revised truth

**Established evidence:** AcMCP explicitly exploits multistep error dependence; the current v2 abstract states asymptotic marginal coverage and a finite-sample coverage-error bound worsening with horizon. This is more precise than older author-page wording about long-run coverage. It targets multi-step point outcomes; using it for cumulative spend still requires an appropriate target construction and validation. [Wang and Hyndman, v2, 2026-02-02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13115v2)

A new delayed-feedback ACI preprint decomposes fixed-delay updates into interleaved sequences and studies how delay interacts with residual persistence. Its simulations show a delay-to-memory diagnostic is not equally predictive for AR(1), volatility clustering and regime switching. This is a promising research challenger, not an established production solution; variable provider delay, selective reporting and billing revisions are not resolved by its fixed-delay result. [El Halabi and Brandt, v1, 2026-09-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07251v1)

**Proposed design:**

- Store forecast origin, target start/end, horizon/remaining-day count, data and mapping vintage, price basis, immutable prediction, outcome maturity rule and outcome version. Maintain a pending-score queue; a 30-day target cannot supply its complete error tomorrow.
- Distinguish first-reported from mature-charge evaluation. Forecasts trained at an old origin must never see later revisions; retrospective evaluation may compare to a declared mature version. Retain both operational and restated scores.
- Updating a truth version replaces/recomputes the relevant calibration evidence through a versioned replay or explicitly justified correction. It is not a second independent observation. Avoid applying an ACI error update twice.
- Daily 30-day rolling sums overlap by 29 days. Daily forecasts of the same calendar month end share the same terminal total. Preserve forecast rows for operational evaluation but group uncertainty estimates by target period and origin dependence; do not present 20 forecasts of one month as 20 independent months. Thinning to nonoverlap reduces reuse but does not prove independence.
- Maturity can be informative: expensive bursts might arrive later than ordinary usage. A calibration set containing only quickly settled items can be biased. Report pending value/coverage and assess delay strata; simple complete-case deletion needs justification.
- Pooling across teams increases data only if their score distributions transfer adequately. Report company/team, zero/burst, provider, horizon, remaining-day, newly launched and delay strata where supported; small strata should show insufficient evidence.

## Rare bursts, probabilities and unavailable outputs

Extreme-value methods extrapolate tail probabilities using structural tail models because relevant events may be rare or unseen. Peaks-over-threshold models need a defensible threshold, sufficient exceedances, treatment of clustered events and checks of stability. Multivariate extremes distinguish asymptotic dependence from independence. **Inference:** a fitted Gaussian copula or an empirical residual pool can underrepresent simultaneous new-project bursts even if ordinary-day fit is strong. **Proposed design:** compare threshold sensitivities and shared-burst stress paths; keep stress scenarios unweighted unless probabilities are defensible. [Davison and Huser, 2015](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-statistics-010814-020133)

For a validated sampled predictive distribution, `p_hat=mean_m 1(total_m > budget)` estimates the model's exceedance probability. Its simulation error is different from model and calibration error. A dense Monte Carlo sample cannot compensate for two historical bursts. A calibrated 90% interval alone does not identify a full CDF or an exact overspend probability; interpolation of a few quantiles is an additional assumption. Distinguish `>` from `≥` when budgets or spend have discrete mass.

Scenario probabilities obey `P(Y>B)=sum_s P(s)P(Y>B|s)` only when scenarios form an appropriate partition and their weights are supplied/estimated. “Approved launch” and extraction confidence do not give `P(s)`. Equal weights chosen for convenience would fabricate risk. Show conditional ranges or sensitivity curves instead.

**Proposed output contract:** retain `target_basis`, `scope`, `period`, `as_of`, `statistic`, `coverage_level`, `coverage_kind` (empirical marginal, theoretical under stated assumptions, scenario, or unavailable), calibration origin/target counts, pending outcomes, truth maturity, method/version, dependence method, validation status and reason codes. Full distribution, intervals, point forecasts and scenarios have separate availability. Permit `insufficient_mature_targets`, `unsupported_financial_basis`, `unvalidated_dependence`, `regime_change`, `unbounded_interval`, and `scenario_weights_unknown`; missing probabilities remain null, not 0 or 50%. Continue showing supported actuals and internal budgets.

## Falsifiable qualification plan

Keep training, calibration/tuning and final evaluation temporally distinct with as-of information. Compare parametric, IID/block residual, direct cumulative quantile/distribution, and dependence-aware path candidates on the same periods. Add conformal wrappers as separate variants. Score mean and median against appropriate losses; measure interval coverage together with width/interval score, upper-quantile loss, budget-event Brier/reliability, and company-period performance. An interval covering everything is calibrated but unhelpful. Evaluate dependence with shared-burst and cumulative events, not marginal scores alone.

Test shift recovery, missing/late billing, growing teams, provider price changes and zero-to-burst transitions; report performance before, during and after shifts rather than one average. Use dependence-aware evaluation uncertainty; an IID binomial interval over overlapping rows is not justified. Separate simulated fault tests from accuracy evidence. The preference for direct cumulative targets or block paths must be overturned if held-out decision-target performance favors another design. No source reviewed supplies representative enterprise AI-spend validation, and no numerical probability threshold is qualified by this research alone.
