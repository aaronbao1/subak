## Established evidence and its limits

1. **Match a prediction to its use.** Point-forecast rankings can be misleading when the loss function does not match the requested functional. Means and quantiles answer different loss-sensitive questions. This supports declaring the target and scoring rule before comparison; it does not select a company's risk tolerance. [Gneiting, arXiv v2, 2010 / JASA 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.0902v2).
2. **Decision error can differ from prediction error.** Smart Predict-then-Optimize defines loss through downstream decisions and uses a surrogate for training. Its optimization structure and experiments establish a method, not superiority for internal AI budgets. It requires specified objectives and constraints. [Elmachtoub and Grigas, v5, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005v5).
3. **Forecast deployment can change outcomes.** Performative prediction formalizes feedback between predictions, decisions and later data. Its convergence results have mathematical conditions; ordinary retraining is not guaranteed to fix arbitrary behavioral feedback. [Perdomo et al., ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/perdomo20a.html).
4. **Observed utilization can censor demand.** Huh et al. study inventory demand observed through sales, using adaptive Kaplan–Meier methods. Their model assumes IID underlying demand; discrete-demand guarantees and specific observation rules cannot simply be transferred to adaptive AI teams. [Huh et al., 2011](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1100.0906), [author manuscript, model §2](https://faculty.marshall.usc.edu/Paat-Rusmevichientong/psfiles/km-myopic.pdf).
5. **Judgment is neither automatically beneficial nor automatically harmful.** Across more than 60,000 forecasts in four supply-chain companies, judgmental adjustments improved average accuracy in three; small adjustments often harmed accuracy and upward changes performed less well than downward changes. This is observational, domain-specific evidence, not a ban on upward enterprise-spend adjustments. [Fildes et al., January 2009](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/effective-forecasting-and-judgmental-adjustments-an-empirical-eva/).
6. **Intervention claims need causal assumptions.** The observational identification framework distinguishes observed associations from interventions and requires conditions including consistency, exchangeability and positivity. Company-wide reallocations also raise interference concerns because teams share resources. [Hernán and Robins, *What If*, indexed April 26, 2024 edition, chapters 2–3 and 13](https://content.sph.harvard.edu/wwwhsph/sites/1268/2024/04/hernanrobins_WhatIf_26apr24.pdf). Access qualification: relevant text was verified through the search index; direct PDF fetch timed out. The author's current download points to an August 19, 2026 edition, which exceeded the browsing tool's size limit; no claim of reading that edition is made.
7. **Robust planning can test vulnerabilities.** Robust decision making iteratively compares strategies across possible futures and identifies conditions under which each fails. The cited application concerns pollution-control policy, not AI finance. [Lempert et al., April 2006](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0472).
8. **Information value depends on consequences as well as uncertainty.** Howard's framework values information through its effects on decisions; resolving two uncertainties jointly need not equal the sum of their individual values. [Howard, August 1966](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4082064/). The publisher's indexed abstract was accessible; direct access presented a browser challenge.
9. **Usage and perceived productivity are insufficient outcome measures.** A 2025 randomized study of 16 experienced open-source developers and 246 tasks found longer completion times when early-2025 AI tools were allowed. This setting-specific result is not an enterprise productivity coefficient. [Becker et al., v2, July 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089v2). The researchers' 2026 follow-up reports selection, task noncompletion, quality differences and concurrent-agent time-measurement problems that make newer estimates unreliable. That is useful counterevidence against treating task completion or self-report alone as ROI. [METR, February 24, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/).

## Inference for this product

The useful artifact is a decision packet: what may happen, on which financial basis, what choices are feasible, what evidence supports them, and what remains unknown. A more accurate forecast can be less useful if it arrives after finance's decision window. A conservative forecast can increase unnecessary reviews or strand funds. A lower provider bill can reflect cancelled valuable work, a different provider, delayed activity, or additional human effort; it is not automatically savings or efficiency.

The product itself does not impose caps, but existing provider controls or voluntary managerial restraint may affect its data. A team that stops at $10,000 may have wanted $10,000 or much more. An internal allocation alone does not establish such censoring. An alert that causes finance to add funds changes the threshold; an alert that causes work to be deferred changes demand. Scoring either against the final revised budget without policy history can reverse the interpretation of success.

## Proposed design: decision quantities and contracts

For team i, one declared period and a compatible financial basis, retain: current allocation B_i; observed incurred amount A_i; a separate estimate N_i of incurred but not yet observed charges; future continuation F_i; and any nonoverlapping fixed-cost component K_i. A period-total target may be Y_i = A_i + N_i + F_i + K_i **only** if the category coverage is compatible and disjoint. Otherwise return the supported components, not an invented total. Billing-date charges and incurred costs require different targets. Internal credits require an explicit mapping before dollar comparisons.

Derived decision views can include expected excess E[(Y_i − B_i)+], probability P(Y_i > B_i), and distribution of remaining headroom B_i − Y_i. Compute them from a supported distribution; a point estimate does not supply probabilities. Distinguish expected excess from max(E[Y_i] − B_i, 0). Show already-incurred excess separately from uncertain future excess. Do not add team quantiles to obtain a company quantile. Calendar-month remainder and cumulative 7/14/30-day targets need exact endpoints; compare only compatible budget periods or use an explicit, approved period mapping.

Each packet should bind `forecast_id`, as-of cutoff, target period/time zone, cost coverage/unit, observed snapshot, policy basis, model/scenario version, context versions and supersession, uncertainty availability, current budget version, and proposal version. Include only evidence actually used, plus sources supporting material narrative claims. The approved research plan can support a workload scenario; it cannot alone support a calibrated multiplier. Extraction certainty that a plan says “expand” is not the probability of expansion or overspend.

A useful warning also declares materiality, expected amount, earliest relevant date, and review lead time. A simple two-action alert model with constant false-alert cost c_FP and missed-event cost c_FN would alert when p > c_FP/(c_FP+c_FN). This is an analytical illustration with restrictive assumptions, not a product default. Real finance losses depend on excess size, timing, review burden and available actions. Elicit those tradeoffs or show a sensitivity range rather than silently choosing P90.

Likewise, minimizing c_short(D−a)+ + c_idle(a−D)+ yields a quantile allocation under a separable one-period model. The unused-allocation term is an opportunity penalty, not an actual expense. Coupled company totals, changing work, thresholds, ordinal priorities, irreversible commitments and unknown value invalidate direct application. Start by comparing feasible finance-authored proposals. Do not mandate decision-focused training or an allocation optimizer.

## Proposed design: policy and scenario semantics

Maintain four distinct meanings:

| Result type | Meaning | Required evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuation forecast | Future spending given recorded current conditions and a stated policy assumption | As-of usage, prices, context and policy history | Deployment or later decisions may change its target |
| Bookkeeping comparison | Change B while holding the same forecast Y fixed | Original allocation and proposed changes | Changed risk margin does not demonstrate changed spend |
| Conditional workload scenario | If specified tasks, timing or model mix occur, corresponding spending under stated assumptions | Workload quantities, cost mapping and explicit assumptions | Scenario spread is not a calibrated interval; weights may be unknown |
| Causal intervention estimate | Expected outcome if a particular allocation policy were imposed | Identifiable intervention and defensible experimental/observational design | Historical association and an LLM explanation do not establish causation |

Policy records should distinguish internal allocation, external enforced constraint, voluntary restraint and unknown status. Preserve announced/effective dates, scope, actor/reason where available, observed enforcement evidence, exception history and decision lineage. Denied attempts, deferred projects or throttling logs can indicate constrained demand, but missing logs do not mean unconstrained demand. A censored-demand estimator is a candidate only when its observation and stationarity assumptions are investigated; otherwise provide a bound or an unavailable latent-demand target.

For Northstar's original synthetic inputs, the two approved example arithmetic transformations remain separate proposal branches. A $30,000 redistribution reduces product to $50,000 without increasing the company envelope. A $50,000 addition leaves product at $80,000 in the supplied example and increases the envelope. Neither operation by itself changes projected spend. A later assumption that funded research accelerates work creates a new scenario and review basis.

Proposal state must distinguish draft, revised, rejected, stale, applied and failed. Rejection leaves saved allocations unchanged and must not be encoded as a zero-allocation action. A backend receipt is the evidence of application; repeated requests must refer to the same outcome. Check budget-version staleness and evidence staleness independently: an unchanged budget does not rescue a proposal whose supporting project has been cancelled. Recompute and present material changes for renewed review; retain the original packet for explanation. The source plans already require explicit finance review; this research adds no outbound messages or external enforcement.

## Proposed design: judgment, robust choices and information value

Preserve numerical baseline, contextual adjustment and finance's risk reserve as separate objects. An adjustment records direction/amount, target, rationale, evidence, expiry, author/actor and the information absent from the baseline. Prevent a planned expansion from entering twice through model features and a manual uplift. Evaluate adjustments by size, direction, novelty, regime and availability at issuance; do not reward adjustment merely because it agrees with a later budget. Reserves express preferences and must not contaminate unbiased forecast evaluation.

Compare a small candidate set—no change, specified redistribution, specified additional funding, or staged review—against transparent scenarios such as launch slippage, research acceleration, cheaper-model substitution with higher volume, and delayed billing. Show where a choice ceases to be adequate and what evidence would trigger reconsideration. When scenario probabilities are unsupported, report scenario-wise consequences and sensitivity, not expected utility with invented equal weights. Minimax regret can be a comparison aid but depends on the scenario set and chosen loss; an implausible extreme can dominate it. There is no universal robust choice.

Under a specified loss L(a,θ), a fixed feasible action set and a usable probability model, perfect-information value is min_a E[L(a,θ)] − E[min_a L(a,θ)]. It is an upper bound on improvement from information that can be ignored; collection cost and delay reduce net value. This formula is a decision-theoretic calculation, not a measurement of company ROI. Initially use a simpler screen: which missing fact could change the choice before the deadline? Confirming whether product's launch moved may be more useful than refining a remote forecast tail. If no feasible action changes over a plausible range, further modeling has limited immediate decision value. Log the range and assumptions so contrary evidence can overturn that conclusion.

## Proposed bounded finance-usefulness study

No study has run. First pre-register a small formative study, for example 8–12 finance practitioners and a balanced set of 12–18 frozen cases, sized for usability discovery rather than a population-effect claim. Use historical as-of cases only when documents, allocations and later revisions are reconstructable; otherwise label coherent synthetic cases. Include stale evidence, incomplete coverage, constrained demand, cancelled work, correlated team shocks and the two funding branches. Counterbalance presentation to reduce learning effects.

Compare (A) actuals plus current budget and a simple run-rate baseline, (B) a calibrated forecast packet, and (C) that packet plus cited context/scenario comparison. Hold feasible actions and available facts constant where testing presentation; use a separate experiment when testing additional context. Record decision time, arithmetic/period errors, identification of stale or unsupported claims, unnecessary review burden, evidence comprehension, confidence calibration, proposed revisions and chosen action. Independent reviewers can check objective constraints; there is no unique “correct” allocation without agreed utilities.

Keep statistical evaluation separate: as-of error, probabilistic calibration/sharpness, coverage and warning lead time. Evaluate utility across a predeclared range of shortfall, idle-allocation, delay and review costs rather than selecting weights after seeing results. Retain no-change and simple-baseline comparators. Group uncertainty by participant and company/period; overlapping horizons and multiple cases are not independent samples.

In a later authorized shadow deployment, freeze issued predictions and log decisions, unimplemented recommendations, effective dates and outcome revisions. Record attempted, completed, abandoned and deferred work; human review/rework time; quality evidence; and provider spend separately. Missing effort or business-value data stays unknown, not zero. No forecast-minus-actual difference is automatically “savings,” and spend under a new policy is not a clean counterfactual for the old policy. Randomized decision support or a defensible causal design is separate future work.

## Unvalidated hypotheses and overturning results

A decision packet may reduce finance review effort; intervals may overload users; context may improve direction but damage calibration; shorter horizons may be accurate yet too late to act. None is established for this population. Retain the simpler baseline if richer packets do not improve comprehension or decisions, increase unjustified confidence, or fail under cost sensitivity. Reject a contextual adjustment strategy if its out-of-time benefit disappears with leakage controls. Reconsider any causal or censored-demand model when overlap, policy evidence or identification fails. The ten proposed fault cases in `edges/18-decisions.json` are specifications, not executed tests.
