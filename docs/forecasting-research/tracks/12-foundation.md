## Established evidence: what is available now

“Zero-shot” below means no task-specific parameter fitting; it does not mean no observed history, no preprocessing, no validation-based hyperparameter selection, or calibrated enterprise probabilities. Jointly forecasting variates differs from batching independent series, pretrained sharing across datasets, and cross-learning across series at inference. Native numeric covariates do not consume planning documents directly.

## Established evidence: benchmarks, cost and generalization

Vendor leaderboards are release-time comparisons with different model versions, covariate availability, context lengths and decoding settings. Chronos-2's code expressly warns cross-learning can hurt accuracy and creates batch-size dependence; its report uses groups around 100. Latest package also requires matching task schemas within list inputs. [Inference source](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting/blob/main/src/chronos/chronos2/pipeline.py), [release changes](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting/releases).

Measured-by-author cost evidence is narrow. Chronos-2 card reports >300 series forecasts/second on one A10G, not a guarantee for many covariates, short batches or CPU startup. Toto card gives forward-pass latency at batch eight and 1024-step single-pass on A100: 4M ~3.8ms, 22M ~5ms, 313M ~15.4ms, 1B ~20.9ms, 2.5B ~36.2ms. These omit endpoint startup, preprocessing, transfer, calibration and pricing. Weight file size is not runtime memory. **This research measured no actual inference cost or latency.** [Toto card](https://huggingface.co/Datadog/Toto-2.0-1B).

Pretraining contamination has degrees. Seeing an evaluation series' earlier history is different from seeing its held-out future; both differ from a genuinely unseen company's series. Moirai 2 explicitly trains on non-leaking historical GIFT-Eval train context plus internal data. TimesFM 3 excludes fev-overlapping datasets from GIFT pretraining and discloses Wikipedia/Trends cutoffs. These disclosures do not audit every deduplication or make AI-spend generalization established. [Moirai training card](https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small/raw/main/README.md), [TimesFM training card](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch).

TIME (ICML 2026, v4 June 3) addresses reused legacy data, quality and task misalignment using 50 fresh datasets/98 tasks; newer checkpoints can still be selected against its public leaderboard. GIFT-Eval-CTX (October 13) supplies verified structured effects **derived from realized future** across 10000 instances/20 datasets. It tests applying informative context, not whether uncertain enterprise plans will occur or can be extracted without hindsight. A good score cannot validate event probabilities or a hiring-plan token multiplier. [TIME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12147), [GIFT-Eval-CTX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17794).

Moirai 2's report explicitly observes worse performance at longer horizons and plateauing with size. Lag-Llama maintainers recommend benchmarking zero-shot first and fine-tuning if needed. Neither foundation pretraining nor larger parameter count settles the project's short-history, sparse-team, changing-price problem. [Moirai report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11698), [Lag-Llama practices](https://github.com/time-series-foundation-models/lag-llama).

## Inference for this enterprise target

A permitted local foundation model may reduce training burden, particularly for many short team/provider/category histories. It cannot reconstruct unreported charges, map vendor projects to owners, infer contract tiers, or predict an unannounced deployment. Forecast quantity remains typed: usage, calculated variable cost, reported charge, or reporting delay. Fixed contractual obligations and conversions remain explicit arithmetic. Native cost forecasting should compete with usage-then-price forecasting because different price regimes can reverse the benefit of historical patterns.

A missing first month is not a zero-spend team; a new team with no history is not ordinary zero-shot transfer. Across reviewed models, no verified guarantee covers history-free enterprise cold starts. Peer-informed prior/scenario forecasting and “unavailable” are distinct candidates. Mask support helps represent missing values but does not identify late reporting or explain why a value is absent.

Multivariate inputs can exploit common work schedules or migration signals, but correlated telemetry and shared keys can create duplicate information. Unrelated teams, nonaligned calendars and ownership changes can cause negative transfer. Cross-company inference groups would also cross data-governance boundaries. A function accepting a batch should never choose those boundaries implicitly.

Most shortlisted outputs are per-step marginal quantiles. A finite quantile grid, including a median, does not identify an exact expected mean: distributions can share those quantiles and have different tail mass and expectations. Averaging reported quantiles is therefore not automatically mean spend. A native point output must declare whether it is a mean, median, or another statistic. Deriving a mean from samples or distribution integration requires declared interpolation/tail assumptions, finite-expectation handling and validation. Quantiles are not joint trajectories across days or teams; summing daily P90 does not yield 30-day P90. Even sampled model paths need dependence calibration. TimeGPT's explicit coupled flag is useful evidence to preserve, not a guarantee of correct enterprise correlations. Masked inputs, output clipping, extrapolated tails and rollout can alter calibration.

## Proposed design and falsifiable selection plan

Maintain a checkpoint-level registry containing source/weight hash, package lock, license snapshot, permitted deployment mode, target schema, feature types, context and horizon behavior, masks, quantile grid, native point statistic, mean availability/derivation assumptions, joint-sample semantics, hardware requirement and documented/verified status. A component requiring daily expected values must reject or explicitly adapt quantile-only outputs; it must never relabel the median or a simple quantile average as a mean. “Multivariate” and “supports features” are insufficient booleans. Pin preprocessing as well as weights. A cloud response should record model identity and service metadata; no silently mutable alias should masquerade as a reproducible checkpoint.

Start evaluation with simple forecast baselines supplied by the statistical track and a **small diverse eligible panel**: Chronos-2, TiRex-2, one t0 checkpoint, and a small Toto 2 checkpoint for histories without required future features. Add TimesFM 2.5 to isolate value of an external-regression design, or TTM for adapted compact modeling. Include TimesFM 3 via permitted cloud serving, TimeGPT and Timestral only after matching actual account access, terms and budget. Noncommercial model evaluation must not imply a deployable winner. This proposal concerns comparison coverage, not expected performance order.

Use held-out companies or teams and rolling cutoffs with event-time and knowledge-time snapshots. Keep three evaluations separate: frozen zero-shot; validation-selected contexts/features/groups without weight changes; and task fine-tuning with an inner temporal split. Tune no decision on final test periods. Compare univariate, logically grouped multivariate, and historical/future-feature variants using the same origins. Include feature ablation, irrelevant covariates, stale/superseded plans, false events, cancellations, and data-lag masks.

Measure cumulative 7/14/30-day and calendar-month-remainder quantity and cost separately, bias, quantile loss, calibrated coverage, interval width, aggregate risk, and downstream allocation loss. Stratify by sparse history, team age, missingness, change points and provider migrations. Report macro team performance alongside spend-weighted results so one large production workload cannot hide small-team failures. A model is rejected if apparent average gains come with unacceptable errors on the targeted planning cases.

Measure cold/warm runtime, maximum resident/accelerator memory, preprocessing, calls per forecast refresh, and total service cost at realistic batch/variate/horizon sizes. Budget inference, tuning and failed/retried requests separately. Self-hosting can keep numeric series within the deployment boundary; that is a deployment choice, not evidence that every package avoids telemetry or has enterprise privacy controls. Hosted retention, residency and incident/audit terms require actual service-contract verification.

**Experiments that could overturn this proposal:** a seasonal-naive or rolling baseline wins the held-out decision loss; explicit price arithmetic dominates direct cost prediction; multivariate groups hurt under ownership churn; numerical features add no benefit once timestamp-safe history is used; tuned classical/global models outperform every foundation candidate; or service overhead overwhelms measured forecast value. Any of these supports narrowing or removing the foundation component.

## Unvalidated hypotheses and remaining unknowns

Transfer from observability may help token-rate dynamics, while invoicing dates and commitment cliffs may behave unlike telemetry. Future plans may help only when encoded as conditional scenarios, not asserted known values. Small models may be sufficient for daily horizons, but parameter count does not prove CPU service latency. No supplied enterprise dataset establishes any hypothesis. No vendor cloud entitlement, quoted price, exact private-service retention, minimum-history threshold, enterprise calibration, or fault test has been validated here.

The accompanying edge register proposes implementation checks, not executed results. Research completion is not completion of the overarching implementation goal.
