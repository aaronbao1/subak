# 07 — Sparse and bursty spending

Fresh research, accessed 2026-10-24. This track concerns human teams and human-initiated tasks. It proposes candidates and tests; no enterprise forecast experiment has been executed. The master plan §§2, 4, 5 and 7 require distinguishing usage, estimated cost, reported charges, subscriptions and incomplete observations. The internal discussion motivates team/task/project variation; the settled master plan keeps autonomous swarm control and provider enforcement outside scope.

## Decision to carry forward

**Proposed design.** Benchmark direct gross variable spending against activity-count/conditional-cost models on the same complete, as-of datasets and cumulative targets. Retain simple averages and seasonal methods in that comparison. Use Croston/SBA/TSB and temporal aggregation as inexpensive candidates, not as an automatic sparse-series routing rule. Gate all occurrence and deactivation updates on coverage. The first model should be the simplest candidate that survives the intended company/team/horizon evaluation; none of the sources establishes a universal enterprise AI-spend winner.

**Inference.** Intermittency is partly a measurement choice. A person's daily task costs may be sparse, a team weekly total dense, and billed overages sparse even while usage is continuous. Forecasting at a finer level can expose plans and task types, but also creates weakly identified components, more attribution errors and more uncertain aggregation.

## Established evidence: what the candidate families actually provide

| Family | Evidence and mechanism | Conditions, burden and failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Croston | Smooth positive amounts and gaps separately; forecast amount/gap. Shenstone and Hyndman show that proposed underlying stochastic models conflict with intermittent-demand properties. Point usefulness does not supply a native, coherent predictive distribution. [S01](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/croston.pdf) | Low burden and a useful rate baseline; long zero runs do not reduce its forecast. Do not interpret its smooth mean rate as a predicted burst date. |
| SBA | Approximately corrects Croston's bias with `(1 − alpha_gap/2) × amount/gap`. Original evaluation compared four methods on 3,000 automotive series. [S02](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/the-accuracy-of-intermittent-demand-estimates/); formula also appears in [S04](https://purehost.bath.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/147092667/JORS_2011_pre_print_.pdf). | Approximate bias correction under the studied setting, not a guarantee for changing AI workloads. Shares the zero-run staleness problem. |
| TSB | Smooth occurrence probability each period and positive size on occurrence; forecast their product. This addresses obsolescence in the authors' simulation framework. [S03](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/145394864/Intermittent_demand_Linking_forecasting_to_inventory_obsolescence.pdf) | With occurrence smoothing beta, probability after k observed zeros is `(1−beta)^k` times its starting value. It can mistake missing feeds, leave or a planned pause for cessation. |
| ADIDA | Aggregate into larger non-overlapping buckets, forecast, then disaggregate. Original empirical study used 5,000 RAF spare-part series. Aggregation reduces zeros while discarding timing information. [S04](https://purehost.bath.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/147092667/JORS_2011_pre_print_.pdf) | Requires enough history after aggregation. Bucket length and disaggregation weights must be chosen inside training folds. It can smooth away a launch or move spend across the month boundary. |
| IMAPA / multiple temporal aggregation | Combine forecasts from several aggregation levels. Petropoulos and Kourentzes study this approach and combinations; the optimal single aggregation level remains problematic. [S05](https://kourentzes.com/forecasting/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Petropoulos-Kourentzes-2014-Forecast-Combinations-for-Intermittent-Demand.pdf). Current StatsForecast exposes IMAPA. [S06](https://nixtlaverse.nixtla.io/statsforecast/src/core/models.html) | More fits and levels, fewer observations at coarser levels. Paper evidence comes from spare parts. Combining levels need not outperform a direct horizon model on changing enterprise work. Pin the eventual library version; current documentation is not a reproducible environment. |
| Poisson / negative binomial counts | Poisson has variance equal to its mean; NB2 has variance `mu + mu²/phi`. [S08](https://mc-stan.org/docs/functions-reference/unbounded_discrete_distributions.html) | Model integer tasks/requests, not dollar amounts. NB can absorb overdispersion, but does not by itself model burst timing, task marks or permanent inactivity. Heterogeneity and unmodeled covariates can produce overdispersion without a distinct inactive class. |
| Hurdle / zero-inflated count | A hurdle assigns all zero mass to an occurrence component and uses a zero-truncated positive count component. A zero-inflated count model also permits zeros from the ordinary count component. [S07](https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/finite-mixtures.html) | Different scientific stories. Many zeros alone do not prove a latent permanently inactive population. Additional mixture parameters can be weakly identified with short histories. For continuous positive spending, use an occurrence-plus-positive-amount model rather than treating dollars as counts. |
| Tweedie / compound Poisson-gamma | For `1<p<2`, a Tweedie model represents a Poisson number of gamma amounts, with zero mass and positive continuous values; variance follows a mean-power relationship. Mean and dispersion may need separate predictors. [S09](https://gksmyth.github.io/pubs/insuranc.pdf) | Direct nonnegative aggregate-cost candidate when reliable task identities are unavailable. Its implied zeros, dispersion and tail shape are restrictive; negative adjustments are outside support. A Tweedie fit does not establish that real tasks arrive as a Poisson process. |
| Renewal / marked arrivals | Discrete-time renewal models allow arrival aging, clustering and quasi-periodicity, and can be generalized to granular event timestamps. [S11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01550v1) | More history, state and diagnostics than a level estimator. Useful only if timing structure survives calendar, exposure and coverage controls; finer timestamps are not automatically more informative. |

The inventory papers optimize demand-estimation or inventory-related outcomes, not our budget shortfall decisions. Their sample sizes are not independent enterprise-company counts. Product type, horizons, losses, observation frequency and stationarity differ. No published benchmark here validates transfer.

## Define the random quantity before the loss function

**Established mathematical facts.** For horizon H, let `S_H = sum_{j=1}^{N_H} C_j` be gross variable cost incurred in that horizon. If marks have a common finite mean and the count is independent of the entire mark sequence, `E[S_H]=E[N_H]E[C]` for finite expected count. The variance identity `Var(S_H)=E[N_H]Var(C)+Var(N_H)E[C]²` additionally requires pairwise uncorrelated marks with a common finite variance and finite count variance; independent identically distributed marks are sufficient. With dependent marks, conditional variance retains their pairwise covariance terms: `Var(S_H|N_H=n,x)=sum_j Var(C_j|n,x)+2 sum_{j<k} Cov(C_j,C_k|n,x)`. The general law is `Var(S_H|x)=E[Var(S_H|N_H,x)|x]+Var(E[S_H|N_H,x]|x)`, when these moments exist. Without count–mark independence, the general mean identity is

`E[S_H|x] = sum_n P(N_H=n|x) × n × E[average cost | N_H=n,x]`.

The term for n=0 is zero; average task cost is otherwise undefined at n=0. An event-weighted pooled severity mean can reproduce an unconditional aggregate mean by a ratio identity even with dependence; that does not recover the dependent predictive distribution or justify transfer to a changed task mix. Conditional frequency-severity GLMs explicitly use count as a severity predictor. [S10](https://doi.org/10.1016%2Fj.insmatheco.2016.06.006)

A zero median is compatible with material expected expenditure. As a mathematical illustration, daily spend of $0 with probability .9 and $1,000 with probability .1 has mean $100 and median $0. With independent identical days, seven-day mean is $700, probability of any spend is `1−.9^7`, about .522, and seven-day median is $1,000. Summing daily medians gives the wrong horizon median. These are illustrative assumptions, not observations or an experiment.

**Proposed design.** Expose which functional is reported: expected cumulative cost, median, quantiles, probability of exceeding an internal allocation, or conditional cost given activity. Mean forecasts suit expected totals; a finance user's allocation loss can call for another functional. Daily MAE can favor zero predictions in a sparse population, while squared error can be dominated by rare costly tasks. Evaluate cumulative mean bias/error and probabilistic calibration separately; a low daily error does not establish useful budget forecasting. Do not use percentage errors with zero denominators.

**Inference.** A smoothed mean cannot promise a specific burst date. A contextual evaluation plan can support a scenario for date/count/task mix; it cannot supply an empirically calibrated event probability simply from an LLM's confidence in extracting the plan.

## Direct spending versus activity and severity

**Proposed design.** Compare three contracts on identical targets:

1. Direct daily or direct cumulative gross variable cost: minimal task metadata, fewer propagated estimates. It absorbs observed task mix but confounds activity, prices and caching; a new price regime can invalidate continuation.
2. Count/occurrence plus conditional task cost: forecasts task starts and their marks, then allocates each task's spending to the target dates. This can explain changes in volume versus cost, but requires stable task identity, partial-task treatment and provider/model pricing. Calls, retries and subtasks are not interchangeable units.
3. Hybrid: accrued cost plus conditional continuation of already active tasks plus new-task arrivals; add separately identified fixed fees and adjustments. This resolves in-flight work but has the largest operational burden. Session architecture is owned by track 16.

A count model with expected staff exposure can separate staffing changes from per-person rates, but active-user days are potentially endogenous: using realized future activity as an exposure leaks the target. Calendar/staffing known as of origin is different from future observed activity. For included usage, gross paid-variable cost can remain zero while task counts rise; predict usage and apply the actual allowance/overage contract, or evaluate a direct cost model that represents the threshold. Do not turn included usage into a second expense.

### Exact dependence investigation to specify before evaluation

**Proposed test, not an executed finding.** Choose an immutable task definition and an incurred-cost basis. For each fully observed team/time cell, record count, positive-cell average task cost, model/provider/task-type mix, exposed staff, calendar and instrumentation version. Audit task cost conservation to provider/usage totals first.

Fit a count model using origin-available predictors. Compare a severity model without count to one conditioned on count and task mix; include shared team/project effects where supported. Predict future count or simulate it, then integrate severity over that distribution. Supplying the realized future count only to the dependent model would invalidate the comparison. On held-out origins, compare horizon bias, proper distribution scores, burst-event errors and budget-exceedance calibration. Report uncertainty in the difference and the number of positive cells.

Inspect within-team/time-regime residual association between count and average severity, not count versus total spend, which contains count arithmetically. Repeat conditioning on task mix: busy days may contain many cheap tasks or a few expensive investigations. A rank association is descriptive, not proof of causal dependence. Use a time-block/bootstrap or explicit temporal model for uncertainty; an exact permutation test is justified only under exchangeability within its declared strata. In sparse strata, absence of significance does not establish independence. An independent model wins if it is better calibrated out of sample or the dependent model's added parameters do not generalize.

## Zeros, survival, bursts and censoring

**Proposed design.** A zero enters an occurrence likelihood only when scope, permissions, pagination, event-time coverage and completeness are supported for the target quantity. Distinguish: observed zero activity; observed activity but zero billable cost; account/project not exposed; incomplete/late observation; and unknown mapping. An empty successful response is insufficient. Provider-level completeness cannot prove an individual's inactivity under a shared key. Freeze or propagate missing-state uncertainty across an outage; never insert observations of zero. Late reported costs require a separate incurred-but-unreported component rather than a future-usage burst.

**Established evidence.** The BG/NBD customer model is an example of jointly modeling transactions and unobserved dropout. It assumes Poisson activity while alive, heterogeneity, and an absorbing inactive state; dropout occurs after a transaction in BG/NBD. [S13](https://www.brucehardie.com/papers/018/fader_et_al_mksc_05.pdf) These assumptions are strong for employees and projects that can pause and resume. Survival likelihoods distinguish completed events from right-censored durations using survival probabilities. [S12](https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/survival.html)

**Inference.** Prefer explicit effective-dated project completion or permission revocation when genuinely known; retain residual accrued charges. Otherwise, disappearance can mean leave, a feed failure, a provider switch, a changed key or real deactivation. A reversible active/paused/finished state with evidence is a candidate, not an established latent truth. An absorbing death model can underforecast reactivation.

**Proposed design.** Open tasks at forecast origin have right-censored final duration and total cost. A closed-task-only sample preferentially includes short runs. Separate accrued spend from future continuation; condition on elapsed time, observed prefix and task state, and evaluate completion selection explicitly. A task crossing midnight or month end must contribute only the costs incurred inside each horizon. Cancellation, externally enforced limits and provider errors are distinct terminal reasons. A capped observed process estimates spend under that cap, not uncensored potential demand. The product itself does not enforce the cap.

Heavy right skew does not establish a power-law tail. Clauset, Shalizi and Newman demonstrate why visual log-log fits or least squares are inadequate and recommend fitted-tail goodness-of-fit and comparisons with alternatives. [S14](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062v2) **Proposed design:** compare gamma/lognormal or other justified positive-size distributions and empirical resampling before tail extrapolation. Keep legitimate large runs in evaluation. Clipping training data or reporting exponentiated log predictions can alter the target mean; transformations require explicit mean recovery and sensitivity checks. Very sparse extremes may support scenario stress ranges while leaving extreme quantiles unavailable. A bootstrap of ordinary history cannot discover unprecedented runaway work.

Refunds, rebates and corrections belong to a signed adjustment process with their own reporting/service-date basis. Fitting Tweedie or gamma directly to net spend either fails or invites silently clipping negatives. Gross $100 and a $100 refund is net zero, not no activity. Keep the components and derive net forecast samples with their dependence and timing; never assume the adjustment is a future usage reduction.

## Falsifiable research sequence and stopping conditions

**Proposed design.** At rolling origins, compare observed-rate/seasonal baselines, SBA/TSB, ADIDA/IMAPA, direct hurdle/Tweedie and count/conditional-severity candidates. Use cumulative 7/14/30-day and exact calendar-month remainder targets as initial candidates, with the same as-of history and time zone. Stratify by complete history length, positive-event count, zero-run length, project state, aggregation level and cost concentration. Train all thresholds, smoothing choices and aggregation weights inside folds. Replay late data and connector failures separately from forecast-skill scoring.

Support probabilistic claims only when the distribution construction itself is specified and calibrated at the cumulative horizon. For temporal/team aggregation, preserve joint draws or calibrate the aggregate directly; do not add quantiles. Cold-start all-zero history must retain an explicit insufficiency state or a justified pooled prior. Historical zero alone cannot establish permanent future zero.

**Unvalidated hypotheses.** Direct team totals may beat granular task models when task identities are unstable; task models may win around known mix/price changes; TSB may help genuine phase-outs but harm temporary pauses; multiple aggregation may reduce noise but miss scheduled bursts. Any recommendation is overturned by sufficiently supported contrary out-of-sample results, including a simple baseline winning. No universal minimum number of positive events, preferred distribution, deactivation threshold or smoothing parameter has been validated here.
