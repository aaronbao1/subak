# Human-initiated coding-agent sessions and continuation demand

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: human teams using coding agents; no autonomous-swarm control design. Findings are fresh literature review and proposed architecture, not executed forecasting experiments.

## Decision and evidence status

**Proposed design:** retain a daily company/team forecast as the minimum viable comparator, enrich it with trustworthy session features, and evaluate an optional near-term continuation model when session identity and live coverage exist. Do not make complete task-cost prediction, an LLM's own token estimate, or a point-process model a prerequisite for useful FP&A forecasting. Compare all three approaches on identical as-of information, targets, and coverage. The short-horizon advantage of active sessions is plausible, not established for this enterprise population.

This respects the master plan's variable-usage focus, explicit team mappings, distinction between usage/estimated dollars/reported charges, and lack of a personal UI commitment. The mentor transcript, especially lines 69–103 and 114–118, asks whether completion-time or resource self-estimation is feasible while advocating a bounded, localized human-team problem. Those questions are not evidence that elapsed time equals tokens or that a budget is an execution cap. Original transcript dates and speakers remain unchanged.

### Established evidence, with limits

* **Benchmark token variability:** Bai et al., *How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money?*, arXiv **2604.22750v3**, revised **2026-10-02**, studies eight models in OpenHands on SWE-bench Verified, with four runs per task. It reports substantial same-task variation, input-driven cost, weak alignment with human difficulty ratings, and weak-to-moderate self-estimation correlation (up to 0.39) with underestimation. This tests pre-execution agent self-prediction, not supervised team forecasts or production workloads. Task/harness/model differences and stochasticity deserve separate treatment; the results do not prove all session forecasts are impossible. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22750v3).
* **Real usage has nested structure:** TraceLab, arXiv **2606.30560v2**, revised **2026-06-30**, observes **4,265 sessions**, **43 developers**, and **357,161 model steps** across Claude Code and Codex. A persistent session contains user requests and repeated model/tool steps; user messages are not identical to new billable steps. It reports long contexts, long-tailed tool delays, and cache misses concentrated around human pauses. The sample comes from researchers' own workflows, with external logs rather than provider internals. Its approximately $40.4K is **API list-price equivalent**, not enterprise billing. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30560v2).
* **Autonomy is not consumption:** Anthropic's **2026-02-18** study measures elapsed autonomous turns, interruption, and approval patterns. It explicitly says that public API calls cannot reliably be grouped into sessions, whereas its own Claude Code product can link workflow activity. Duration confounds task selection, product speed, human oversight, and concurrent subagents. Neither a duration percentile nor METR's human-task-duration metric supplies a token-to-time conversion or a spending forecast. [Primary study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy).
* **Budget awareness is not forecast accuracy:** Ramp's **2026-04-21** experiment uses GPT-5.4 with mini-swe-agent, a 50,000-token initial budget, and a selected 100-task controller set balanced by whether continuation succeeds. Passive budget prompts did not observably change reported usage metrics. A controller given oracle success probabilities made the intended choice; it did not learn those probabilities. The article's prose claiming near-universal approval by four models conflicts with its displayed class accuracies, so that stronger claim should not be repeated. These are control experiments under a particular utility rule, not empirical validation of aggregate spend forecasting. [Primary study](https://labs.ramp.com/research/coding-agents-ignore-spend/).
* **Prefixes offer information, not guaranteed calibration:** BAGEN, **2606.00198v1**, **2026-05-29**, evaluates remaining-budget intervals on rollout prefixes across four environments and five agents, finding optimistic errors and weak interval coverage. Its replay estimates and early-stop experiments concern eventual remaining task resources and feasibility. The replay does not establish realistic future session arrivals, business-horizon spending, or live intervention effects. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00198v1).
* **Compaction changes future behavior:** Satish et al., **2609.32961v1**, **2026-09-26**, varies compression policies across three open-weight models and two coding benchmarks, covering almost 35,000 runs. Reducing tokens did not consistently reduce elapsed time or estimated cost; effects depend on task and model. Consequently, a fixed savings multiplier for compaction is unsupported. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32961v1).

## Define the forecast before choosing a session model

**Proposed target contract:** for team g, forecast variable incurred usage or its explicitly priced estimate in the interval **(t,t+h]**, conditional on observations available at t. The 7/14/30-day and month-remainder targets remain candidates; an hour/day continuation view is a component or diagnostic, not an unrequested replacement for finance horizons. Usage can be a vector of chargeable token categories. Apply time-valid pricing only where coverage supports it; preserve unknown monetary values. Fixed subscriptions, included usage, and commitments belong to their separate accounting components.

Let request j start at T_j and let C_j(u) be its cumulative incurred usage by calendar time u. Each invocation is assigned exactly once, including attributable child work. Define the horizon increment:

`Y(t,h) = sum_{T_j <= t} [C_j(t+h)-C_j(t)] + sum_{t < T_j <= t+h} C_j(t+h)`.

Here C_j is zero before request start. The first term is continuation of existing requests; the second is the portion of newly arriving requests executed before the boundary. Completed requests contribute zero to the first term. A new human request in an old conversation is a new arrival, with inherited-context state. A human clarification that resumes a paused request is a continuation if the source explicitly links it; a timestamp heuristic cannot settle that ambiguity alone.

**Inference:** eventual task severity `C_j(infinity)` cannot be put entirely into the request's start day. That wrongly advances tomorrow's tokens into today and overstates month remainder when a task crosses month end. Predicting remaining total cost and predicting when that cost occurs are separate problems. Also keep a third component, late-incurred usage missing from the telemetry at t, separate from genuinely future continuation. If usage is only reported at response completion, retain its available occurrence interval and timestamp convention rather than silently inventing a uniform consumption path.

Cost already observed is not residual forecast. A failure, cancellation, timeout, or abandoned task can still incur charges. Conversely, a tool waiting for a user or a long test run may consume no new model tokens until it resumes. “Open session,” “active autonomous request,” “in-flight model call,” and “potentially resumable session” therefore require different states.

## Alternative models and what they require

| Candidate | Information and assumptions | Benefit | Main burden/failure | Test that could overturn its use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily team model with session features | Reliable daily totals; lagged counts, active-user exposure, observed model/mode mix, concurrency summaries | Works without detailed linkage; naturally targets finance periods | Aggregation loses current unfinished work; future covariates may be unknown | No improvement from session features on strict rolling-origin tests |
| Arrival-count × conditional severity | Request IDs, starts, marks, partial/complete trajectories; count/severity dependence modeled | Explains whether demand changed through activity or cost per request | Closed-only sample bias; severity drifts with harness/cache; timing missing | Joint model fails to beat direct daily totals with comparable information |
| Marked point process with continuation | Fine timestamps, event triggers, state history, identities, reliable coverage | Models intraday arrivals, overlapping work, event timing, correlated bursts | High implementation and calibration cost; fake excitation from telemetry batching | Calendar/count baseline matches accuracy and calibration at lower cost |
| Near-term prefix model plus future-arrival component | Current request state and recent usage, plus a separately defined target for future arrivals | Conditions on work already underway without predicting every future task | Double counting if daily model already covers continuation; stale snapshots | Removing active-prefix component does not hurt hour/day or finance-horizon scores |

**Proposed starting sequence:** fit a pooled daily baseline; add coarse session summaries; only then fit conditional continuation. Preserve a model-free fallback whenever identifiers or telemetry disappear. If forecasts require observed future model mix, they are scenarios, not unconditional forecasts.

**Hybrid partition invariant:** the active component covers only future increments from requests started by t; the arrival component covers only increments from requests started after t. Train/evaluate each on those disjoint labels, with known late-incurred usage assigned to the separate nowcast. A daily total forecast already contains continuation and must remain a standalone comparator; adding a full active forecast to it is invalid. If no trustworthy partition exists, use a daily model with active-state features rather than additive components. Require component sums to reconcile to the same covered total target on every replay cutoff.

For counts, compare weekday/hour/exposure-adjusted Poisson to negative-binomial or hierarchical counts. A nonhomogeneous intensity λ_g(s | history) can represent schedules, staffing exposure, adoption, known deployments, and observed recent activity. Marks may include task class, model/version, effort, mode, harness, and prefix state. A marked process produces both event times and conditional resource increments.

**Established mathematical scope:** Hawkes processes model events whose arrivals raise future arrival intensity. [Laub, Taimre and Pollett, 1507.02822v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02822). **Unvalidated hypothesis:** this might describe follow-up coding requests, but weekday schedules, common releases, shared incidents, or ingestion batching can create apparent excitation. Do not infer causal contagion or adopt Hawkes simply because usage is bursty. Compare calendar-conditioned Poisson/Cox-process alternatives and inspect residual dependence. Recursive autonomous delegation is a different population and remains parked.

Count and severity cannot generally be multiplied as independent averages. For new arrivals, conditional totals require `sum_n P(N=n|x) * E[sum_{j=1}^n W_j(h)|N=n,x]`, where W is each arrival's within-horizon contribution. Large incidents may cause both more requests and more expensive ones. Simulate or jointly model shared team/day shocks; derive company quantiles from summed joint paths, never from adding team or session quantiles. Session correlation also matters when the same human retries one task in several windows.

## Prefix survival, censoring, and concurrency

**Proposed continuation states:** running model, waiting tool, waiting human, resumed, completed, cancelled, failed, and unknown due to telemetry loss. Preserve source-observed transitions separately from inferred states. A practical first challenger is a discrete-time transition model plus conditional next-step resource marks. Compare it to empirical matched-prefix residual distributions and parametric survival/reward models before introducing a complex neural event model.

For an uninterrupted request with age a and duration survival S, the residual duration probability is `P(D>a+u | D>a,x)=S(a+u|x)/S(a|x)` under the stated covariates. Time-varying prefix state requires updated conditional modeling, not blindly reusing that baseline ratio. Resource prediction additionally needs the joint relationship between duration and token intensity: a long wait is different from a long reasoning run. **Established methodology:** time-dependent survival covariates must use information available just before each interval; they cannot incorporate eventual outcomes. [Therneau, Crowson and Atkinson, dated 2026-01-12](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/survival/doc/timedep.pdf).

Training must include live requests that remain unfinished at extraction: their terminal duration and final severity are right-censored. Requests begun before coverage are left-truncated if their age is known; if age is unknown, mark it unknown rather than setting start to first visible event. A successful stop, user cancellation, and provider cutoff are competing terminal causes, not interchangeable censoring. Missing instrumentation may depend on costly runs or application crashes, making independent censoring doubtful; use observed coverage covariates and sensitivity analyses rather than claiming standard survival guarantees automatically apply.

Build landmark snapshots at fixed elapsed or clock times using only prefix observations. Keep every prefix from a request—and related retries/forks where known—in the same split. Do not train on final step count, ultimate success, future compaction, end-of-run token rate, or task classifications extracted from the completed conversation when evaluating a start-time forecast.

Concurrency must be computed as overlap of state intervals, not number of files updated recently. Track currently running requests, in-flight model calls, tool waits, and human waits separately. Different sessions can compete for one person's attention; this can reduce new human arrivals while existing agents continue. Shared quotas can delay or abort parallel calls, moving incurred usage across the horizon. These are candidate covariates and scenarios, not facts identifiable from generic usage totals alone.

## Minimum instrumentation and architectural handoff

**Proposed append-only contract:** tenant/source/event IDs; provider request/invocation ID where supplied; source and observed-at timestamps; occurrence interval; human/session/request/parent/fork identity with linkage provenance; team mapping effective time; model identifier and alias-resolution version; harness/version; mode and reasoning effort when available; usage category and counter semantics; terminal reason; tool/model/human wait state; coverage interval; and schema version. Missing dimensions remain missing, not default values.

**Live-continuation capability gate:** the source manifest must separately expose `supports_daily_totals`, `supports_session_summaries`, `supports_identified_prefixes`, `supports_live_state`, and availability-time provenance. Aggregates and finalized session summaries do not establish live prefixes. Enable continuation only with identified prefixes, adequate state coverage, and an explicitly validated freshness policy; stale or incomplete state routes to the daily fallback. Historical ingestion timestamps are required to claim arrival-time replay.

Keep cumulative usage counters separate from context length. A compaction can reduce context without reducing incurred usage. Counter resets need explicit epoch handling; a fork may inherit context or copied log history without creating a new historical bill. A retry with a distinct provider invocation can incur new usage even if its semantic task is identical; an exact replayed event must not. Store model switches as step-level changes, not a single label copied from the final state. Keep cached input and reasoning inclusion semantics source-specific before normalizing.

Task type can be a low-cardinality, as-of semantic feature such as bug fix, review, research, or generation, with provenance and unknown state. Do not require raw source code or hidden reasoning for basic forecasting. A classifier's confidence is not a probability of overspend. User identity may support partial pooling internally without creating individual budgets or ranking UI. Team ownership comes from the company's effective-dated mapping, not inferred repository names.

The backend supplies reconciled, coverage-aware events and state snapshots; forecasting owns numerical transitions and aggregate distributions; Factory may extract task/context signals with references. No component may transform a forecast into provider enforcement. A physical conversation and a logical task need not be identical, so normalized events must remain usable for daily aggregation even when task linkage fails.

## Feasibility, evaluation, and open hypotheses

The existing 40-file feasibility sample demonstrates possible telemetry, not sufficient training diversity, tail coverage, or billing validation. TraceLab's metadata supports conditional public pipeline/prefix experiments, with stable pseudonyms and no raw conversation text. Its repository distinguishes **Apache-2.0 code** from **CC BY 4.0 public data** and identifies release **v0.0.1**. Pin that asset/checksum and the extractor/schema separately; the current README may describe newer parsing behavior. Neither public traces nor benchmarks supply enterprise billing truth. [Repository](https://github.com/uw-syfi/TraceLab).

**Field-level availability is essential:** the README documents Codex usage reports arriving after tool execution and tool-result association varying with turn-ID/CLI availability. A normalized round is not wholly observable at its first timestamp. At each cutoff, mask final token totals, result status/size, and tool latency until their supporting report/result event; preserve availability provenance for derived fields. A tool emission does not reveal its eventual result. Without original ingestion history, label this **event-time prefix simulation**, not preserved source-delay or arrival-time replay. [Data format](https://github.com/uw-syfi/TraceLab#-data-format).

**Proposed evaluation:** use chronological as-of replay with field availability and recorded source delays preserved where those data exist; otherwise report event-time simulation and explicit delay assumptions. Hold out developers/projects/harness versions where possible; compare daily-only, feature-enriched daily, session, and hybrid candidates on identical team and company targets. Score cumulative means/medians and interval calibration at 7/14/30 days and month remainder, alongside hour/day component diagnostics. Report errors separately for existing continuation, future arrivals, late-incurred nowcasts, known price conversion, and uncovered usage. Measure operational overhead and forecast inference spend. Block bootstrap or group resampling should respect related requests and shared calendar shocks.

There is no defensible universal minimum session count. Feasibility depends on observed starts, terminal outcomes, independent users/projects, duration tails, state transitions, and stable versions—not just millions of token events. Use learning curves and uncertainty in held-out calibration to decide whether partial pooling or an aggregate fallback is warranted. Require empirical improvement large enough to justify linkage and maintenance costs.

**Unvalidated hypotheses to preserve:** (1) active prefixes add the most value within hours, with diminishing contribution over finance horizons; (2) observed model/mode mix explains more variation than human task difficulty; (3) common incident/project shocks dominate individual session dependence; (4) updated cache and harness state may matter more than elapsed duration; (5) pooled session models can transfer across teams only after coverage and identity harmonization. Any can be rejected by evaluation. No selected source establishes a universally best enterprise architecture or an enterprise spending accuracy number.
