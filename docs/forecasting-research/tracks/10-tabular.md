# 10 — Global tabular forecasting: regression, GAMs, forests and boosting

Research checked 2026-10-24. This is a candidate design and experiment specification; no model was fitted, no enterprise accuracy established, and no runtime measured. Scope follows `docs/ai-spend-master-plan.md` §§1–3 and backend evidence handoffs: company/team finance planning, human teams and human-initiated agents, explicit units and coverage. The mentor transcript questions representativeness, company differences and usefulness of short horizons (`mentor-discussion.txt`, lines 29–54); these are evaluation requirements, not reasons to assume a sophisticated model wins.

## Decision and evidence boundary

**Proposed design:** retain pooled regularized regression and one modest global boosted-tree family as serious challengers to the classical baselines. Start with a common point-in-time feature table and direct cumulative targets for 7/14/30 days and calendar-month remainder, while comparing recursive daily forecasting. Add a quantile challenger only after the point model and target basis are credible. CatBoost is a practical first challenger when categorical identities dominate; LightGBM is equally credible when throughput and explicit categorical control dominate. These priorities are engineering hypotheses, not measured rankings.

**Established evidence:** global forecasting can share a function across series, including heterogeneous groups. Montero-Manso and Hyndman formalize expressivity and generalization differences and demonstrate competitive global linear models. Their results do not guarantee that a finite, constrained model pools this customer's teams well. [Locality and Globality, v3, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00444)

**Established counterevidence:** M5's winning retail methods strongly support testing boosted trees with covariates; however, only 415 of 5,507 submitted methods (7.5%) beat its strongest benchmark. The benchmark population, substantial history and known retail covariates differ from enterprise AI spend. Success of selected winners is not success of an arbitrary LightGBM configuration. The uncertainty winner also emphasizes leakage, overfitting and time-series validation. [M5 accuracy results, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2021.11.013), [M5 uncertainty winning method, 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169207021002090)

## Target and feature contract

**Proposed design.** A row is `(tenant, series definition, forecast origin, target window, target basis, snapshot version)`. A series might be team × provider × model-family usage, team variable estimated cost, or team reported incurred charges. Do not combine these labels merely because all are numbers. Fixed subscriptions, minimum commitments, included usage, credits/refunds and allocation budgets are separate components. A predictive budget feature is not permission to change a budget.

For a nonnegative variable-spend series, define `S(i,t,h) = sum(y(i,t+k), k=1..h)` after the agreed local-day boundary. Record exact target start/end and timezone; month remainder has a variable horizon. An incomplete current day, charges incurred earlier but still unreported, and future continuation require separate explicit components. Preserve finalized-label vintage and availability time. Calendar sums are not necessarily cash-billing sums.

The minimal feature groups are:

- **Past observations:** lags 1/7/14/28 days; strictly trailing means/sums over 7/28 days; active-day fraction; time since positive activity; sample count; incomplete-day flag; collection delay and coverage. All rolling windows end at the last information actually available at origin. A centered window, unshifted target-inclusive rolling mean, or later corrected history is forbidden in a historical feature snapshot.
- **Known future calendar:** horizon length, weekday composition, local holidays when relevant, workdays and month-end. Historical plans must use the version available then, not their ultimately realized dates.
- **Exposure and composition:** observed active seats, authorized planned staffing, model/tool shares, project phase and scheduled work if available. Distinguish active users from provisioned seats and future planned values from realized future counts. Rates need valid exposure denominators; zero exposure is a state, not a divide-by-small-number hack.
- **Identity/metadata:** stable team/project/provider/model-family keys, age since activation, coarse function and organizational context. History can be modeled at individual granularity without exposing individual UI or promising that sparse individual histories improve team forecasts.

**Established evidence:** scikit-learn's lagged-feature example obtains an overly optimistic error from shuffled train/test splitting. This supports temporal evaluation; it does not specify the correct split for delayed financial labels. [Lagged features example, 1.9.1](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/applications/plot_time_series_lagged_features.html)

**Inference and proposed safeguards:** at fold origin `T`, every training feature must have been available at its own origin, and every training label must have become available by `T`. For cumulative targets, an origin before `T` can still have a target window extending past `T`; drop it. Also exclude labels whose reporting/revision maturity is later than `T`. Use whole-origin temporal folds across all series so a contemporary company aggregate cannot reveal a held-out team's target. Fit encoders, imputation, scaling, category grouping, selection and calibration inside folds. A general `TimeSeriesSplit` applied to arbitrarily interleaved rows is insufficient.

## Candidate families and their failure modes

| Family | Why include it | Assumptions, burdens and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Linear/ridge/elastic net | Fast pooled lag/calendar benchmark; shrink correlated lags and identity coefficients; inspect coefficients and stability. | Requires encoding and scaling; additive unless interactions supplied. Extrapolating trend can help or catastrophically overshoot. Elastic net variable selection does not make coefficients causal. |
| Poisson/Tweedie GLM | Positive conditional mean through a log link; meaningful exposure/rate formulation. | Objective must match target support. Conditional mean modeling does not by itself deliver a validated forecast distribution. Standard scikit-learn Tweedie regression uses L2 regularization. |
| Additive spline model / GAM | Smooth time, adoption-age or exposure effects with a compact auditable structure; a bridge between linear models and trees. | Start with spline basis plus penalized GLM, not an extra framework. Specify cyclic periods and extrapolation behavior. Interactions must be explicit; many knots can overfit short history. |
| Random forest | Low-tuning nonlinear challenger; native multioutput is useful for a horizon vector. | Standard squared-error leaves average observed labels, so it cannot extrapolate level beyond training target range. Large forests increase artifact/memory size. Tree spread is not automatically a prediction interval; ordinary random-bootstrap OOB accuracy is not temporal validation. |
| Histogram gradient boosting | Convenient nonlinear and quantile baseline within scikit-learn. | Still needs the same temporal feature construction and calibration. Library convenience does not solve missing target/coverage semantics. |
| CatBoost | Native categorical handling and ordered boosting reduce particular categorical/boosting prediction-shift mechanisms. | These mechanisms do not repair future-derived features or noncausal snapshots. High-cardinality identities can still memorize history; prefer CPU first and test unseen categories. |
| LightGBM | Efficient histogram boosting; explicit categorical regularization; mean, Poisson, Tweedie and quantile objectives. | Leaf complexity must be constrained on small panels. NaN versus zero and category coding are operationally consequential. Never treat arbitrary ID integers as a meaningful ordered scale by accident. |
| XGBoost | Strong alternative with histogram training, count/Tweedie/quantile objectives and integrated tooling. | Extra dependency is justified only by an ablation or deployment need. Multioutput support remains documented as experimental/limited; choose mature one-model-per-target APIs unless tested. |

The regularization and GLM claims are supported by [scikit-learn linear models](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/linear_model.html). Cyclic/extrapolation options are explicit in [SplineTransformer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.preprocessing.SplineTransformer.html). Forest mean leaves and native missing-value routing are documented in [RandomForestRegressor](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.RandomForestRegressor.html); the extrapolation limitation follows directly from averaging those leaves. CatBoost's narrower leakage claim comes from the [2018 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/14491b756b3a51daac41c24863285549-Abstract.html). Objective support is documented by [LightGBM](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Parameters.html), [CatBoost](https://catboost.ai/docs/en/concepts/loss-functions-regression) and [XGBoost](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/parameter.html); multioutput caveats come from [XGBoost's tutorial](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/multioutput.html).

**Proposed scale/cold-start policy:** compare raw-unit loss with scale-normalized targets using an origin-only scale and a documented floor. Evaluate both spend-weighted company error and team-balanced error: a million-dollar team can otherwise dominate squared loss while tiny series dominate relative losses. Retain the scale as a feature; an all-zero history must not imply a confidently zero future. For a new team use metadata/exposure and an eligible pooled fallback, then mark its weaker evidence. Hold out entire teams, and tenants only if cross-tenant training is authorized, to test this claim. Compare full identity, coarse metadata and identity-free variants; do not force transfer when local baselines win.

**Established implementation detail:** LightGBM treats NaN as missing by default, whereas `zero_as_missing=true` also treats actual/sparse zeros as missing. In pandas categorical prediction, unseen categories become missing. **Inference:** a model can still emit a number after collector failure or team creation, so model success is not data validity. [LightGBM advanced topics, 4.7.0](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Advanced-Topics.html)

## Horizon strategy and probability outputs

| Strategy | Benefit | Failure mode / comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Recursive one-day model | One model; naturally produces a daily path; easy to apply calendar/price scenarios. | Later lags are forecasts, creating error propagation and train/serve distribution differences. Never substitute realized future lags in backtests. |
| Direct model per daily horizon | No recursive lag substitution; horizon-specific relationships. | Many models and fewer mature examples at long horizons; independent outputs can be temporally inconsistent. |
| Horizon-conditioned pooled direct model | Share samples across `(origin,horizon)` rows; manageable model count. | Replicated origins do not become independent samples. Horizon lengths need weights and origin-grouped validation. |
| Direct cumulative target | Align loss and quantiles with the finance decision; includes cross-day dependence implicitly in the target. | Loses daily shape; separate horizons may violate cumulative monotonicity or differ from daily-path sums. Needs explicit consistency checks. |
| Multioutput horizon vector | Shares representation and possibly split structure across horizons. | A vector of point outputs is not a joint predictive distribution. Complete label windows reduce usable recent training data; library/objective support varies. |

**Mathematical fact:** daily conditional means given the same forecast-origin information add by linearity of expectation. A nonlinear recursive model that feeds predicted means back as lags does not generally produce those multi-step conditional means: if the next state depends on the square of a random earlier state, squaring its mean differs from its mean square. Call such outputs recursive point forecasts. Mean semantics require direct horizon-specific conditional-mean predictions, or averages from valid stochastic recursive simulations that propagate the state distribution. Joint draws must retain temporal and cross-entity dependence when aggregate risk is reported. Marginal quantiles generally do not add. Direct cumulative quantile models therefore avoid the invalid sum-of-daily-quantiles shortcut, but do not produce joint team/day scenarios or guarantee calibrated budget-exceedance probabilities. Independent daily sampling also discards common shocks and serial dependence. Joint scenario/reconciliation work belongs with the uncertainty and hierarchy tracks.

**Established evidence:** NGBoost jointly learns parameters of a chosen conditional distribution using natural gradients and a proper scoring rule. This is more expressive than a mean-only tree, but remains conditional on distribution family and covariates; its 2020 tabular results are not temporal enterprise-spend validation. [NGBoost paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/duan20a.html)

**Proposed distributional sequence:** compare direct quantiles at 0.1/0.5/0.9, measure pinball loss and empirical coverage by origin/horizon/scale/cold-start state, and check crossing. Sorting/rearranging quantiles is a separate postprocessing candidate whose scores must be remeasured. Compare NGBoost Normal only where support/tails are defensible, or a positive distribution only on genuinely positive targets. Zeros cannot be deleted to fit a positive distribution; a hurdle model needs an occurrence component and conditional positive-size component. Negative refunds belong in a separate financial component. A count objective can model token/request means, but claiming a Poisson full distribution assumes mean/variance behavior often implausible for bursty work. Tweedie with power between one and two accommodates zero plus positive continuous outcomes; it is a useful loss candidate for nonnegative variable costs, not a proof that billing follows a compound-Poisson mechanism. CatBoost explicitly warns that large Tweedie labels can overflow; scale and finite-output tests are necessary. [GLM support and zero mass](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/linear_model.html), [CatBoost regression objectives](https://catboost.ai/docs/en/concepts/loss-functions-regression)

## Affordable experiments that could reject the recommendation

**Proposed bounded screen, not tuned settings.** Freeze the same data vintage, eligible origins and evaluation criteria first. Use three historical validation origins if history supports them, then reserve untouched later origins. Short history may make this screen inadmissible; return a baseline/unavailable state rather than claim validation. CPU only initially; report actual wall time, peak memory, artifact size and eligible-row counts. Allocate equal trials, not an exhaustive cross-product.

| Candidate | Initial small grid / cap |
|---|---|
| Ridge/elastic net | Standardized origin-safe features and normalized target; ridge alpha `{0.1,1,10}`; elastic-net alpha `{0.001,0.01,0.1}` × L1 ratio `{0.1,0.9}`. Nine configurations; scales make these proposals interpretable, not optimal. |
| Spline GLM | 4 or 8 knots on selected numeric features; L2 `{0.1,1}`; fixed calendar encoding. Four configurations for one support-valid family. |
| Random forest | 200 trees; min leaf `{10,30}` × feature fraction `{0.5,1}`; cap depth at 12. Four configurations. |
| First booster | Six predeclared combinations spanning depth 4/6/8 or leaves 15/31, conservative leaf/group regularization, learning rate 0.05 and at most 500 rounds; chronologically separate early stopping. Do not multiply every setting. |
| Quantiles | Add three quantiles only to the best eligible simple/boosted structures; retain baseline intervals. If separate estimators are required, count all three fits. |
| Distributional/second booster | At most four follow-up configurations after a concrete residual/support failure or meaningful first-stage result; otherwise stop. |

Run the initial screen for a horizon-conditioned model to avoid multiplying the full grid by every horizon. Compare the two best eligible structures under direct cumulative versus recursive daily construction next. Explicitly cap the entire screen by a user/project compute budget before execution; no duration estimate is supported by this research.

**Overturning ablations:** (1) remove identities and context separately; no improvement means their complexity is unjustified; (2) whole-team cold-start holdout versus familiar-team temporal tests; divergent results can require a fallback; (3) raw-unit versus normalized/team-weighted fit with both company and team metrics; (4) direct cumulative versus sum of recursive point forecasts and properly simulated path aggregates; (5) strict historical vintages versus finalized-history oracle, reporting the latter only as leakage sensitivity; (6) shorter training window versus all-history under tool adoption/price changes; (7) remove headcount/exposure and price features to distinguish coverage artifacts from useful signals; (8) evaluate sparse, high-spend, new-model and collector-outage slices. Reject boosting if its advantage vanishes across held-out origins, damages an important slice, or fails the agreed operational budget. Synthetic data tests software and known mechanisms, not enterprise forecasting efficacy.

## Interpretation and operations

**Established boundary:** SHAP explains predictive associations; it does not justify interventions. **Proposed product wording:** “the model used recent activity and project timing” rather than “moving allocation caused lower spend.” Coefficients, split gain and permutation importance can all be distorted by correlated lags and identity proxies. Use held-out temporal importance, grouped feature ablation and provenance to debug; an allocation scenario needs explicit causal or scenario assumptions. [SHAP authors' causality guidance](https://shap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/example_notebooks/overviews/Be%20careful%20when%20interpreting%20predictive%20models%20in%20search%20of%20causal%20insights.html)

**Observed package inventory, accessed 2026-10-24:** [scikit-learn 1.9.1](https://pypi.org/project/scikit-learn/) (2026-09-10, BSD-3-Clause), [CatBoost 1.2.10](https://pypi.org/project/catboost/) (2026-02-18, Apache-2.0), [LightGBM 4.7.0](https://pypi.org/project/lightgbm/) (2026-07-18; [repository MIT license](https://github.com/lightgbm-org/LightGBM)), [XGBoost 3.4.1](https://pypi.org/project/xgboost/) (2026-08-15, Apache-2.0), [NGBoost 0.5.11](https://pypi.org/project/ngboost/) (2026-06-26, Apache-2.0). XGBoost stable documentation identified itself as **3.4.2**, a discrepancy with the observed PyPI release; verify installed-version compatibility before adopting options. These are observed registry/docs versions, not an installed or approved lockfile. Paper/dataset reuse rights are separate from software licenses; this track did not establish dataset redistribution rights.

**Proposed operational minimum:** serialize feature schema, target/unit definitions, cutoffs, encoders, normalization scales, package lock, seed, selected parameters, validation results and training source lineage with each artifact. Pin CPU threading to avoid oversubscription; preserve a baseline fallback. LightGBM's PyPI instructions require OpenMP on macOS; CPU/GPU objective support differs (CatBoost MultiQuantile is documented CPU-only). Prefer one deployable model family over maintaining all contenders. Serving should reject schema/unit/category-map incompatibilities and distinguish stale, incomplete and unsupported predictions. New models, changes in team attribution, pricing and missingness are monitored drift events, not excuses to silently refit on corrupted data.

**Unvalidated hypotheses remaining:** a sparse enterprise panel contains enough repeatable dynamics for pooled trees; planned work adds forecast signal beyond recent usage; a direct cumulative formulation improves finance-relevant error; normalized pooling helps small teams without hurting company totals; and conditional quantiles remain calibrated through adoption shifts. Only prospective or carefully reconstructed historical evaluation can resolve them.
