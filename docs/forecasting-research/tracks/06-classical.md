# 06 — Classical forecasting for enterprise AI spending

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: research and proposed experiments only; no models fitted and no enterprise accuracy established. This review was constructed from the master/workstream plans, original transcripts, and freshly accessed sources, without consulting the archived dossier.

## Decision and requirements

Retain a genuinely competitive classical slate: calendar/run-rate methods, ETS, seasonal ARIMA, dynamic regression, structural state-space, Bayesian structural models, and a bounded Gaussian-process challenger. There is no evidence that the simplest or most complex member wins for this population. Model eligibility, out-of-sample cumulative loss, uncertainty calibration, operational reliability, and useful context sensitivity should determine deployment.

The master plan requires finance-facing company/team forecasts, clear source/coverage/units, and separation of fixed subscriptions, included activity, variable billable usage, calculated costs and reported charges. Its Northstar projections are placeholders. Factory may identify a research expansion but cannot supply an invented token multiplier. The mentor transcript explicitly distinguishes missing-bill estimation from future spending and questions adoption shocks, company-specific seasonality and horizon usefulness (lines 22–29, 38–54, 102–118). The original discussions motivate individual-level analysis, but the settled initial interface remains company/team level. These are requirements and concerns, not statistical evidence.

**Proposed target contract.** Fit a homogeneous daily incurred variable-cost or usage series with documented ownership and service-date basis; forecast each supported quantity separately. Add scheduled fixed charges under an explicit recognition basis, without adding included usage again. Treat late-incurred amounts through a separate nowcast component. A cumulative forecast is `recorded incurred + late-incurred nowcast + future continuation + applicable fixed components`, with no overlap. Price changes are separate known-effective-date transformations or scenarios; a raw dollar trend cannot explain whether consumption or price changed. Predicting individual usage is an experimental granularity choice, not an individual-budget feature.

## Established evidence, with limits

1. **ETS and ARIMA represent different structures.** Automated ETS chooses among innovations state-space models; automated ARIMA searches differencing and autoregressive/moving-average structures. They are not interchangeable labels for a moving average. Information-criterion selection within a family is not evidence of population-wide superiority. [Hyndman and Khandakar, author-hosted article preprint](https://www.robjhyndman.com/papers/forecastpackage.pdf).
2. **Extrapolated trends require judgment.** Undamped Holt trends continue indefinitely; damping reduces the long-run slope. ETS multiplicative-error variants require strictly positive training observations. Neither property establishes calibrated enterprise spend tails. [Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, exponential smoothing](https://otexts.com/fpp3/exponential-smoothing.html).
3. **Calendar variables are useful model inputs, not evidence of their effect.** Weekday counts, interventions and distributed lags can be represented explicitly. The effect size must be learned or labeled as a scenario. An actual deployment at midnight and an approval dated midnight are different events. [Time-series regression](https://otexts.robjhyndman.com/fpp3/regression.html).
4. **Regression forecasts require future predictor values.** Calendar dates can be known; future activity, actual headcount and project execution generally are not. Standard regression-with-ARIMA-error intervals condition on supplied predictor paths and omit uncertainty in those paths. Harmonic regression offers compact seasonal bases but fixed coefficients imply repeated shapes. [Dynamic regression](https://otexts.robjhyndman.com/fpp3/dynamic-regression.html).
5. **Transformed forecasts need the right estimand.** Inverse-transforming a symmetric transformed mean generally gives an original-scale median, not an expected cost. Means add; medians generally do not. Joint original-scale paths also preserve temporal dependence for cumulative totals. Rolling-origin multi-step assessment is distinct from in-sample residual fit. [Forecaster’s toolbox](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html).
6. **Complex seasonal models exist.** TBATS combines transformations, Fourier seasonal states, trend and ARMA corrections, including multiple/noninteger periods. That is support for a capability, not evidence that several months of AI history reveal annual seasonality. [De Livera, Hyndman and Snyder, 2011](https://robjhyndman.com/publications/complex-seasonality/).
7. **Structural models permit changing latent components.** Local level/slope, seasonal components and regressors can be composed and estimated in state-space form. A filtered latent level is an estimate, not an observed driver or causal explanation. [statsmodels UnobservedComponents, documented version 0.15.0](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.tsa.statespace.structural.UnobservedComponents.html).
8. **Bayesian structural models can integrate model uncertainty.** Scott–Varian combine structural components, sparse regression and posterior averaging. Their examples emphasize short-term economic nowcasts using contemporaneous predictors; they do not establish future-project spend forecasting. [Scott and Varian, June 28, 2013 manuscript](https://people.ischool.berkeley.edu/~hal/Papers/2013/pred-present-with-bsts.pdf).
9. **GP extrapolation is governed by its covariance and mean assumptions.** Gaussian-process regression yields joint predictive distributions; noisy future observations require observation variance beyond latent-function uncertainty. Standard dense Cholesky training costs cubic time in observation count. [Rasmussen and Williams, 2006, chapters 2, 4 and 8](https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RW.pdf).
10. **Counterevidence to a simple-method winner exists.** M4’s leading methods included hybrids and combinations. Its 100,000-series benchmark population and horizons differ from enterprise AI usage, so neither its ranking nor its reported improvement transfers directly. [M4 results, authors’ institutional record](https://pure.unic.ac.cy/en/publications/the-m4-competition-results-findings-conclusion-and-way-forward/).

## Comparative interpretation

The following are **inferences**, not enterprise validation.

| Family | Conditions that could favor it | Principal failure modes and burden |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar run rate | Recent stable scale, strong work-week pattern, limited history; cheap refresh and intelligible assumptions | Ignores serial shocks and planned changes; choosing the recent window is consequential; holiday effects may be unsupported |
| ETS, including damped trend | Gradual level/slope/seasonal changes with few explanatory inputs | Learns billing batches as seasonality; adapts late to a genuine step change; damping may underpredict sustained adoption; low-to-moderate fitting burden |
| ARIMA/SARIMA | Repeatable short-lag dependence and weekly structure after appropriate differencing | Search instability, excess differencing, weakly estimated seasonal terms, explosive long-horizon uncertainty; moderate per-series search burden |
| Dynamic regression/ARIMAX | Stable measured relationship to known calendar/exposure or explicitly assumed project drivers, plus residual dependence | Future-regressor leakage, collinearity, unknown execution, spurious trending relationships; moderate statistical and substantial feature-governance burden |
| Structural state-space | Slowly changing level/slope, incomplete observations with an explicit observation model, interpretable decomposition | Components confound each other; a one-off shock can become persistent level; uncertainty depends on variance assumptions; moderate fitting burden |
| Bayesian structural | Short/sparse histories with defensible priors, regularized predictors and full posterior paths | Prior dominance and poor identifiability; posterior uncertainty still excludes omitted mechanisms; convergence and sensitivity checks raise burden |
| GP | Modest-length dense histories with plausible smooth/periodic relationships; flexible joint covariance | Kernel-determined extrapolation, bursts smoothed away, hyperparameter overfit, omitted observation noise; high dense-training cost as history/granularity grows |
| TBATS/harmonic extensions | Repeated, demonstrably complex seasonal patterns across adequate historical span | More cycles/parameters than evidence, irregular annual calendars, expensive search; no default annual component for short history |

Daily modeling retains weekday mix, holidays, price-effective dates and partial-month boundaries. Weekly aggregation may reduce day noise but destroys information needed for a Tuesday-to-month-end forecast and leaves only about thirteen observations from three months. A weekly model must either predict aligned full weeks only or supply a separately evaluated daily allocation model; dividing a weekly total by seven is an assumption. More daily rows do not create more independent evidence when observations are strongly correlated.

For short horizons, latest level and weekly pattern may dominate; for longer horizons, trend, exposure, price and scenario assumptions accumulate. This is a hypothesis to test separately for cumulative 7/14/30 days and calendar-month remainder. Moving holidays, differing team calendars, weekend research runs and globally distributed staff prevent a universal Monday–Friday zero-cost calendar. An annual cycle cannot be identified merely because the software accepts period 365.25.

### Multivariate and additive decomposable challengers

**Established evidence.** VAR jointly models a vector of outcomes using lagged vectors and an innovation covariance matrix; ordinary stable VAR assumes stationary inputs, with VECM available for cointegrated systems. [statsmodels VAR documentation, 0.15.0](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/vector_ar.html). Prophet exposes Fourier seasonality, holiday effects and additional regressors; its documentation warns about unconstrained seasonal behavior when forecasting portions of a cycle never observed. [Prophet seasonal/regressor documentation](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/seasonality,_holiday_effects,_and_regressors.html), [non-daily observations](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/non-daily_data.html).

**Inference and proposed admission.** VAR/VARX is materially different from separate dynamic regressions: it jointly propagates endogenous team/project trajectories and correlated innovations, potentially capturing work shifting from research to engineering. With K series and p lags, the lag matrices alone contain K²p coefficients; many teams and thirteen weekly observations make unrestricted estimation fragile. A budget sum constraint or accounting reassignment can also generate apparent cross-prediction without transferable behavior. Admit C10 for small coherent panels: K=2..5 homogeneous series, p in {1,2}, calendar exogenous terms, at least 20 complete observations per equation coefficient as a provisional gate, stable fitted dynamics and synchronized coverage. Compare unregularized VARX with ridge penalties {0.1,1,10} on training-standardized lag coefficients, unpenalized intercept/calendar, chosen on inner origins. Innovation draws share a covariance matrix; test calibration rather than assuming dependence is correct. Differenced/logged variants need original-scale joint reconstruction. Defer unrestricted high-dimensional VAR, and defer VECM unless domain evidence plus long history supports stable cointegration; team transfers do not establish it. Future noncalendar VARX inputs still need scenarios.

Admit C11 as a Prophet-style challenger for histories with plausible piecewise trends: linear growth, no daily seasonality for daily aggregates, weekly Fourier order {1,3}, no annual term before the annual eligibility gate, changepoint prior scale {0.01,0.1}, at most five candidate changepoints evenly placed in the first 80% of the training span, holiday prior scale 1, and the same vetted exogenous paths as C5. Compare no changepoints and no exogenous effects. These are proposed settings, not library defaults or accuracy claims. [Prophet changepoint documentation](https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/trend_changepoints.html) explains its sparse rate-change construction. Annual Fourier terms and TBATS belong to the same broad multiple-seasonality investigation, but changing seasonal states (TBATS), fixed harmonic coefficients (DHR) and penalized piecewise trend (Prophet-style) encode different persistence assumptions. The additive challenger must also pass gross-spend support and cumulative-distribution checks; a smooth decomposition does not establish causal explanations. Current Prophet documentation describes nested regressor models beginning in v1.4.0; version-pin and test their uncertainty propagation and origin-time fitting before relying on that capability.

## Proposed candidate definitions

The definitions below are a preregisterable starting slate, not implementation or endorsed defaults. Tune every listed choice using inner historical origins only; evaluate frozen candidates on outer origins. `y_t` is a homogeneous, coverage-qualified incurred series. `z_t=log(y_t)` is eligible only when all modeled observations are strictly positive; no arbitrary epsilon or `log1p` unit choice. Signed credits/refunds require a separate target or a deliberately signed model. Parameter estimates, transformations, training cutoffs and failed fits must be retained.

| ID | Exact initial candidate |
|---|---|
| C0 — reference rates | Constant daily arithmetic mean over last 7 or 28 complete days; seasonal-naïve repeats the most recent complete seven-day vector. These are separately scored candidates. No annualization of one partial day. |
| C1 — calendar rate | For each weekday, arithmetic mean over last 4 or 8 complete weeks; future mean is the mean for that weekday. The probabilistic variant resamples complete historical seven-day vectors aligned by weekday, with replacement in weekly blocks; it retains observed zeros and within-week dependence. No unsupported holiday adjustment. |
| C2 — exposure rate | For each weekday `d`, `r_d=sum(y_t)/sum(e_t)` over eligible same-weekday days in last 8 weeks; forecast `r_d*e_future`. Exposure is explicitly staffed/eligible seat-days, never future observed active usage. A zero denominator makes this variant unavailable. Evaluate unpooled `r_d` versus pooled rate; future exposure is conditional/scenario input. |
| C3 — ETS | Additive-error ETS with trend in `{none, additive, damped additive}` and seasonal component in `{none, additive period 7}`. Fit on raw and, where eligible, log scale. For raw strictly positive data also test multiplicative-error counterparts with admissible fits. Freeze candidate constraints and choose by inner validation; do not compare transformed and raw likelihood criteria without the required transformation treatment. |
| C4 — SARIMA | `(p,d,q)(P,D,Q)[7]`, `p,q in 0..2`, `P,Q in 0..1`, `d,D in {0,1}`, `d+D<=1`, `p+q+P+Q<=4`; also nonseasonal variants. Stationary variants may have intercept; once-integrated variants compare with/without drift. Fit raw/log eligible variants, reject nonconvergent/invalid fits; AICc may nominate within a common response/likelihood basis, then inner cumulative validation chooses. |
| C5 — dynamic regression | `z_t = intercept + six weekday indicators + beta_trend*t + x_t beta + n_t`, where `n_t` is ARMA(p,q), `p,q in 0..2`, `p+q<=3`. Compare calendar-only, calendar+exposure, and calendar+exposure+validated-event features. Remove rank-deficient terms. A separate differenced branch differences target and regressors consistently before ARMA fitting. Fit raw as a distinct signed/additive challenger. Event effect estimates require repeated historical evidence; novel events remain scenarios. |
| C6 — structural MLE | Observation `z_t=level_t + weekday_t + x_t beta + epsilon_t`; level evolves with optional slope. Compare local-level versus local-linear-trend, deterministic versus stochastic period-7 seasonality, and no exogenous variables versus the C5 vetted subset. Estimate component variances by likelihood; compare no slope with slope damped at fixed `phi=0.95` and `phi=1`. Avoid simultaneously adding redundant weekday indicators and a full seasonal state. |
| C7 — Bayesian structural | Standardize eligible log targets using training-only mean/SD. Use local level with damped slope (`phi=0.95`), deterministic weekday terms and optional C5 covariates. Initial level `N(0,1)`, slope `N(0,0.1^2)`; innovation SD priors half-normal scales 0.1 for level and 0.01 for slope; observation SD half-normal scale 1; training-standardized regression coefficients `N(0,0.5^2)` with one reference weekday omitted. Repeat with innovation scales halved/doubled. These are explicit proposed priors, not learned truths or a reproduction of Scott–Varian. Diagnose posterior computation before using draws. |
| C8 — GP | Eligible log target with constant mean and kernel `Matérn-3/2(time) + periodic(time, period=7) + white observation noise`; compare a linear mean variant and a product periodic×Matérn variant for evolving weekly structure. Fix the weekly period, fit amplitudes/length scales by marginal likelihood using five deterministic restarts, then choose variants by inner validation. Produce joint noisy-observation draws. Bound study to <=730 daily observations per fit; this cap is an experimental cost policy, not a statistical theorem. |
| C9 — complex seasonality | Only when eligible long history shows additional repeatability: dynamic harmonic regression with weekly harmonics `K7 in {1,2,3}` plus annual `K365 in {1,2,3}`, ARMA errors as C5; compare TBATS with periods `{7,365.25}`. Moving holidays remain explicit regressors/scenarios. Compare against weekly-only variants; never activate annually merely from an API default. |

**Distribution rules.** C0 is initially a point-reference only. C1 block resampling cannot generate unseen severity and is itself a candidate uncertainty model. Raw Gaussian models can assign negative gross-spend probability; measure it and do not conceal it through unlabeled clipping. Log-Gaussian models imply positive support but cannot represent genuine zeros. For an exactly normal log forecast, the original-scale mean is `exp(mu+variance/2)`; with mixtures/parameter uncertainty, average inverse-transformed posterior/predictive draws. Multiplicative ETS terminology alone does not guarantee every simulated Gaussian-innovation path remains positive. Zero-heavy series should enter explicit intermittent/hurdle candidates from the specialized track rather than be forced into log models.

For each joint future path, first convert usage using that path's price assumptions, then sum across the exact requested dates. Quantiles of daily forecasts must not be added. Team correlations are a separate joint/reconciliation problem; independent per-team simulations do not establish company tail risk. Fixed known charges shift each path once; uncertain contract/seat changes need their own path inputs.

## Eligibility and experiment ladder

These are **proposed design gates**, subject to empirical revision: C0 needs its stated complete window; C1 needs four complete occurrences of each weekday; local seasonal ETS/ARIMA/structural candidates begin at eight complete weeks; regression needs full-rank design and at least ten usable observations per free regression coefficient as a conservative screening heuristic; annual candidates begin after two full annual cycles. Gates do not promise reliable estimation. A missing source day is not a zero; partial days are censored/exposure-limited observations. No-history teams may use separately evaluated pooled priors or scenarios rather than fabricated local seasonality.

1. **Reconstruct information available at each forecast origin.** Freeze usage revision vintage, known calendar, mapping, prices, accepted plans and feature versions. Evaluate late-reporting nowcasts separately from future continuation. Reject origins with unknowable target coverage instead of silently filling them.
2. **Run the full eligible slate on identical outer origins.** Daily origins and horizons 7/14/30 plus month remainder; training windows 28/56/112 days and expanding, where eligible. Inner origins choose windows/structures. Also compare weekly models on genuinely identical aligned targets. Include expanding-versus-recent history under adoption changes.
3. **Score the decision target.** Report cumulative original-scale MAE/bias and squared error for expected totals; quantile loss/weighted interval score and empirical coverage at 50/80/95%; budget-exceedance calibration where outcomes are sufficiently numerous. Stratify by horizon, weekday origin, history length, zero share, spend size, source completeness, team and regime. Count failed fits and unsupported outputs, and measure runtime/memory. Use paired origins; handle overlap/serial dependence in uncertainty on comparisons.
4. **Ablate mechanism and information.** Compare calendar vs no calendar, slope vs damping, exogenous features vs same model without them, deterministic vs uncertain covariate paths, raw vs transformed distribution, daily aggregation vs native weekly. A context feature is useful only if it improves prospective outcomes beyond contemporaneous leakage.
5. **Challenge candidates with the proposed fault register.** Price-only breaks, staffing+holiday coincidences, partial billing revisions, weekend regime shifts, refund contamination, trend extrapolation, negative support, uncertain project execution and weekly/month-boundary mismatch must be exercised before selection.
6. **Choose a frontier, not a predetermined champion.** A model earns deployment through meaningful cumulative accuracy/calibration benefit relative to its cost and failure rate. Retain combinations as separately validated competitors; no assumption that averaging always helps. Keep a safe available reference and explicit unavailable status when a selected fit fails.

**Unvalidated hypotheses and overturning evidence.** C1 may be competitive on stable human-team weeks; persistent lag structure could favor C4. C5/C7 may benefit from planned staffing/project evidence; no prospective gain or worse calibration overturns that expectation. C6/C7 may adapt better to drift; adaptation lag or overreaction to invoice revisions would argue against them. C8/C9 may capture complex patterns; worse cumulative forecasts after holidays or regime shifts would reject them despite attractive fitted curves. No licensed enterprise data or population evaluation was available in this research stage, and no numerical forecast, runtime comparison or uncertainty guarantee is claimed.
