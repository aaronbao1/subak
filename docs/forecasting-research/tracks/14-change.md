# 14 — Structural change, adaptation, and adoption uncertainty

Research date: 2026-10-24. Status: fresh primary-source review and proposed design; no forecasting experiment or product implementation performed. Source identifiers resolve in `sources/14-change.json`; proposed fault tests are in `edges/14-change.json`.

## Decision and requirements

**Proposed design.** Treat change handling as four separable jobs: verify observation coverage, detect a distributional change, investigate possible causes, and choose a forecast response. Preserve a stable baseline alongside adaptive alternatives. A detector result must neither claim a business cause nor authorize an internal budget change.

The master plan requires local company/team planning, distinguishable usage and cost evidence, current versus superseded context, and explicit unavailable forecasts. The mentor transcript (supplied date “Oct 23”) challenges the assumptions that enterprise adoption leaves several weeks to adapt and that replacing a price table resolves a new-model transition. The internal transcript raises changing task/project usage, model mix, and human-initiated agents. These are research questions, not established facts. This track does not assume gradual adoption, predict unannounced model releases, or extend scope to provider enforcement.

## Established evidence and its limits

| Method | What the source establishes | Application, burden, and failure boundary |
|---|---|---|
| BOCPD | Adams and MacKay infer a posterior over time since the last change, then integrate run-specific predictions. Their original model assumes conditionally independent, identically distributed observations within segments and independent segment parameters; a hazard specifies change timing. | Useful for uncertainty over recent history. Likelihood, prior, hazard, and pruning require choices; seasonal/autocorrelated spend does not automatically satisfy the model. “Exact” is conditional on the specified model, not verified detection probability in this population. [BOCPD, v1, 2007](https://arxiv.org/html/0710.3742v1). |
| CUSUM | A reference shift and decision threshold trade in-control average run length against detection speed. NIST contrasts sensitivity to small versus large mean shifts under the chart assumptions. | Low operational burden for standardized residual streams with interpretable thresholds. Calibrate separately for bursts, calendar structure, changing variance, and many teams; a mean detector can miss a tail-risk change. A fixed chart threshold is not a universal financial materiality threshold. [NIST CUSUM ARL](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc3231.htm). |
| ADWIN | The inspected author manuscript assumes independent bounded observations and compares subwindow means, growing or shortening history. Its false-positive bound is **per time step** under stated conditions, not a lifetime or organization-wide guarantee. ADWIN2 compresses the window. | Attractive for bounded quality rates and appropriately specified losses; raw dollar values are unbounded and serially dependent. Clipping changes what is monitored and can hide extreme exposure, so retain a separate unbounded cost-risk channel. Version and test parameters matter. [Bifet–Gavaldà author manuscript, 2006; shorter conference version 2007](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~gavalda/papers/adwin06.pdf). |
| Robust Bayesian detection | Altamirano et al. propose diffusion-score-matching generalized Bayesian updates with formal robustness and conjugacy under specified conditions; their examples illustrate ordinary BOCPD confusing outliers with changes. | A candidate if false resets caused by isolated bursts dominate. Robustness can also downweight a financially important real job, so operational exposure reporting must remain independent. The published speed comparison is not a production benchmark for this product. [ICML 2023 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/altamirano23a.html). |
| Hidden regimes | Hamilton models discrete latent states and state transition probabilities. Filtered state probabilities use observations available now; smoothed probabilities use later data. Few transitions make richer transition models difficult to estimate. | Suitable when recurrent evaluation/development phases have enough examples. Short histories, arbitrary regime labels, and unseen states undermine identification. Never use smoothed state labels in an as-of backtest. [Hamilton manuscript dated 2005](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jhamilto/palgrav1.pdf). |
| Short versus long histories | Pesaran–Timmermann show that retaining some pre-break observations can improve forecast error through a bias–variance tradeoff, including cross-validation and window combinations. Eklund et al. compare rolling, averaging, EWMA, and monitored-break methods; rankings depend on the break process. | Detection is not a sufficient reason to erase history. Maintain recent, longer, and forgetting alternatives. Their regression/macroeconomic results motivate comparisons; they do not establish enterprise AI-spend superiority. [Pesaran–Timmermann, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2006.03.010), [Bank of England WP406, 2010](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2010/forecasting-in-the-presence-of-recent-structural-change). |
| Adaptive experts | Dynamic Weighted Majority changes expert weights, adds experts, and removes experts based on performance. Its evaluation concerns classification and concept drift. | The transferable idea is a bounded set of recent/long/contextual forecast experts, not a claim that the classification algorithm directly solves probabilistic spending forecasts. Regression loss, uncertainty combination, and delayed-label updates require their own specification and tests. [Kolter–Maloof, 2007](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/kolter07a.html). |
| Delayed feedback | Joulani et al. show that feedback delay changes online-learning regret under specified stochastic/adversarial settings, and construct delay-aware reductions. | A forecast cannot learn from an incurred charge that is still unobserved. Record pending outcomes and apply updates when the correct target matures. Their regret bounds are not a calendar-day detection SLA or enterprise forecast calibration guarantee. [ICML 2013](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/joulani13.html). |

## Inference: a spending break has several possible causes

An aggregate amount is insufficient to identify mechanism. A useful accounting representation is variable estimated cost `sum(quantity × effective unit price)` over provider/model/version, token category, tool and other billable meter. Keep fixed fees, reported charges, discounts/commitments, and credits separately defined by the financial contract. This is a descriptive decomposition, not causal identification. Anthropic's current documentation illustrates differentiated cache operations, model pricing, tool-token overhead, and server-tool charges; the exact rules must be versioned rather than copied into a permanent forecasting assumption. [Anthropic pricing, accessed 2026-10-24](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing).

**Proposed design.** Maintain a change-evidence vector at available granularity, plus denominator and coverage checks:

| Candidate cause | Evidence to compare | Consequence for forecasting |
|---|---|---|
| Activity volume | Active people, sessions, calls, task arrivals, jobs, and usage totals | Update activity continuation; separate new adopters from higher usage by existing adopters. |
| Work intensity | Calls/session, tokens/call by category, reasoning mode, retries, task duration | A stable headcount need not imply stable spend; completed-task denominators are optional and require consistent definitions. |
| Model/routing mix | Exact model version, routing weights, default settings, deployment/channel | Reprice each component and preserve uncertainty about workload substitution. A marketing “intelligence tier” alone loses important metering distinctions. |
| Price/contract | Effective dates, price basis, volume tiers, discount and commitment changes | Change the cost transformation without labeling unchanged activity as adoption; renegotiated invoices may affect prior periods. |
| Cache/tool/configuration | Cache reads/writes/misses, schemas and prompt versions, tool-call counts, execution mode | Separate cache warm-up pulses from persistent configuration changes and token overhead from external tool fees. |
| Population/project composition | Team mapping versions, hires/departures, project phases, ownership transfers | Compare stable cohorts and current organizational totals. Team shifts can cancel at company level. |
| Observation/coverage | Connector health, export scope, received/event timestamps, missing partitions, new meters | Incompleteness can mimic a decrease; newly covered activity can mimic growth. Preserve both a comparable panel and full current coverage. |

Several causes may coexist. Record `candidate_causes[]` with evidence and counterevidence; permit `unresolved`. Correlated announcements are not proof that the announcement caused observed usage. A price/quantity counterfactual should identify its order or symmetric decomposition and interaction term: allocating the interaction differently changes the explanation, not the observed total. Zero denominators produce unavailable ratios, never silently zero intensity.

## Proposed adaptation contract and lifecycle

For each series/target store source and meter coverage, event-time interval, available-at timestamp, price/mapping/configuration version, forecast origin, detector version/state, candidate change interval, candidate cause, evidence references, and any data revision. Distinguish `data_quality_change`, `statistical_change_suspected`, `business_change_supported`, and `forecast_adaptation_applied`; these are separate attributes rather than a required linear certainty ladder.

1. **Gate on data fitness.** Do not train an activity drop from a missing export. Known price changes can update rated-cost scenarios even before enough post-change activity arrives. Unknown model prices yield an explicitly partial monetary target while usage remains usable.
2. **Monitor several channels.** Use coverage-health checks, standardized seasonal forecast errors, activity/mix features, and a separate absolute financial exposure channel. Choose monitoring cadence from data availability; hourly monitoring of daily finalized charges does not create hourly feedback. Simultaneous benign shifts require an organization-level alarm budget and grouping of related alerts.
3. **Compare responses.** Keep an unchanged champion, recent-window contender, exponentially forgotten contender, and (when justified) a regime/intervention contender. Candidates include a temporary level pulse, persistent step, ramp, slope change, seasonal-pattern change, or variance change. Context can identify a candidate effective date or relevant feature; it does not supply an arbitrary numerical multiplier.
4. **Update reversible state.** Prefer partial state adaptation or champion/challenger evaluation over destructive deletion. A level reset need not reset seasonality; a price update need not reset activity; a new coverage scope needs a new basis. Preserve artifacts sufficient to replay a revision and restore a prior candidate. Planned and observed changes must not be counted twice.
5. **Score only available targets.** Maintain pending forecast-outcome pairs. A 30-day cumulative forecast cannot receive a realized full-horizon loss tomorrow. Late revised charges require an explicit score revision policy, not another independent training event. Use the same as-of information set for every contender.
6. **Communicate decision uncertainty.** Show an altered forecast with its assumed continuation and unresolved mechanism. If a shock invalidates calibration, flag the limitation and show conditional scenarios or unavailability; do not relabel arbitrary widened bounds as calibrated coverage.

**Inference.** Billing availability, aggregation, detector evidence accumulation, and model learning all contribute to operational adaptation delay. Some overlap; their sum is not generally a theorem. Record incident-to-first-observation, first-observation-to-alert, and alert-to-useful-forecast separately. No universal one-week or two-week response guarantee follows from these sources. Repeated changes faster than the feedback loop may prevent identification altogether.

## Adoption, substitution, rebound, and unannounced events

**Established evidence.** Bass's original model concerns initial purchase timing driven by innovation and imitation, tested on eleven consumer durables. It supplies a possible diffusion shape, not evidence for the speed of enterprise model switching. [Bass, 1969, original title retained](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.15.5.215). More directly relevant evidence comes from a randomized workplace-tool rollout: Dillon et al.'s v4 reports 66 firms/7,137 workers; weekly use initially peaked, and use differed substantially by firm. Tool access was distinct from active use. That study measures workplace behavior rather than token bills or the future path of coding-agent adoption. [Dillon et al., v4, 2025-11-13, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11436v4).

**Proposed design.** Represent at least access/eligibility, activation, continued use, usage intensity, and model substitution separately. Compare a diffusion curve only where there is an identifiable eligible population and meaningful first adoption. Include immediate centralized default switches, phased deployment, exploratory spikes followed by abandonment, repeated activation, and reversals. A renewal that grants seats to everyone can change access in one day; workflow adoption may move differently. Do not constrain the scenario engine to monotonic S-curves.

For a potential new model/tool, use a labeled conditional scenario with effective date, affected cohort, substitution share, task volume, per-task metered usage, cache behavior, price, and rollout shape. Unknown future releases cannot be learned simply by observing prior releases; unspecified scenario probabilities must remain unspecified. A continuation forecast conditional on today's configuration and an “immediate switch” stress scenario answer different questions.

**Unvalidated hypotheses.** Lower per-token prices may induce more experiments, higher-quality models may reduce retries, longer reasoning may increase tokens while reducing task count, and moving to self-hosting may move cost outside the connector. None has a universal sign for company-wide spending. Avoid imposing an elasticity or treating cheaper unit rates as savings. For a homogeneous hypothetical workload, a 50% price reduction with 3× usage implies 1.5× variable spend; this arithmetic is not an empirical elasticity. With several meters, evaluate the full joint scenario. Moving spend outside observed scope is not evidence of lower all-in costs.

## Falsifiable evaluation and operational choice

**Proposed experiments, not executed.** Replay event-time streams with the original arrival and revision history. Include stable periods, step/ramp/pulse/recurrent regimes, changed tails with unchanged means, and mixed faults from this track's edge register. Evaluate actual cumulative 7/14/30-day and calendar-month-remainder targets only where those targets and coverage are available. Injected scenarios test behavior and sensitivity; they do not establish real population frequency or forecast accuracy.

Compare: no detector plus fixed rolling baseline; long/short window selection; fixed equal-weight combinations; exponential forgetting; CUSUM-triggered candidate updates; ADWIN on justified bounded streams; BOCPD with likelihood/hazard alternatives; and a small hidden-regime model only where history supports it. Known interventions need a separate comparison with uncertain execution dates and cancellation. Keep the same information and tuning budget, and tune only inside each training origin.

Report false resets per stable team-month, detection delay measured from observable evidence, missed changes, reversal/recovery time, forecast error and interval/quantile scores before/during/after change, material budget-threshold decision errors, and compute/state burden. Stratify by change magnitude, persistence, coverage completeness, seasonality, feedback delay, and cohort size. Cost-weighted losses supplement average percentage metrics that fail near zero. Report alarm grouping and cross-series false alarms; a detector winning a pointwise benchmark can still lose the finance decision.

**Recommendation that can be overturned.** Start evaluation with a small reproducible set of short/long/forgetting experts and explicit coverage/price logic; add a detector only if it reduces material forecast/decision loss at an acceptable false-reset rate. Reject that recommendation if a simpler baseline wins across representative teams, or a well-specified regime model consistently improves both adaptation and calibration without unacceptable operational cost. No source reviewed validates a universal winner for this enterprise population. Necessary missing evidence includes actual as-of billing histories, enough transitions and reversals, known coverage changes, and representative human-initiated workloads.
