# Task-trained neural forecasting for enterprise AI spending

Research date: 2026-10-24. This is a fresh, independent review of primary papers and inspected official source code. No model training, benchmarking, deployment, or enterprise-accuracy verification was performed. Paper versions and reuse status are recorded in `sources/11-neural.json`; proposed fault checks are in `edges/11-neural.json`.

## Decision contribution and scope

**Inference:** Task-trained neural models merit a controlled challenger track when there is a sufficiently broad panel of comparable workloads, repeated behavior, and reliable covariates. They are not yet justified as the default for this product: actual enterprise histories remain unverified, and three synthetic teams or a small local session sample do not establish learnable enterprise structure. Neither model age nor public leaderboard rank resolves that gap.

**Proposed design:** Compare a compact global MLP family (N-HiTS or TiDE), a probabilistic recurrent model (DeepAR), and one covariate-rich alternative (TFT or TSMixer-Ext), alongside strong statistical and tabular baselines. Add PatchTST/iTransformer only when long histories or cross-variable information create a specific hypothesis. TimeMixer and TimeXer are useful targeted challengers, not obligatory production dependencies. This sequence can be overturned by held-out enterprise results, including results favoring any newer architecture.

The governing requirements are the master plan's separation of usage, estimated cost, provider charges, fixed subscriptions and included usage; its team-facing finance decisions; and its prohibition on inventing numerical effects from contextual plans. The forecasting boundary deliberately leaves methods open and allows unavailable outputs (`docs/ai-spend-master-plan.md`, sections 2, 6–7). The transcripts motivate company-specific behavior and contextual signals; they do not supply training data or an empirical neural-model advantage. This track concerns supervised task-trained models; zero-shot foundation models and textual reasoning are separate comparisons.

## Established evidence: what the families actually provide

“Global” means parameters are learned across multiple series; “multivariate” means inputs/outputs interact across variables. These are independent choices. A globally trained univariate model may learn shared calendar patterns without jointly modeling the company's teams. A model called multivariate may still produce only point forecasts. All families can in principle be fitted locally, but doing so removes pooling and changes both data demands and cost.

| Family | Established architectural distinction and outputs | Conditional enterprise applicability; inference rather than demonstrated spend performance |
|---|---|---|
| **DeepAR** | A shared autoregressive RNN predicts likelihood parameters and generates complete future sample paths. The original formulation assumes covariates available throughout the forecast horizon; categorical identifiers and time features support pooling. The paper studies Gaussian and negative-binomial likelihoods. [DeepAR v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.04110v3) | Useful when many related series and calibrated cumulative uncertainty matter. Count likelihoods suit counts, not dollar amounts merely because currency has cents. Choose a likelihood matching support, zeros and tails; check probabilistic fit. Recursive sampling has horizon/sample-dependent latency. Within-series paths support sums over days; shared weights alone do not create cross-team dependence. |
| **N-BEATS** | Deep fully connected residual blocks generate backcasts and forecasts. Original experiments address univariate point forecasting. Generic and trend/seasonality-constrained variants differ; the interpretable variant provides a mathematical decomposition. [N-BEATS v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437v4) | Strong history-only challenger where pooling can exploit repeated shapes. Original N-BEATS is not a general context-ingestion interface; use an explicitly implemented exogenous extension or another model. Its trend/seasonality components do not identify hiring or project causes. Large ensembles add inference and training cost. |
| **N-HiTS** | Multirate pooling and hierarchical interpolation form forecasts at multiple scales. Its approximation argument requires smoothness; reported efficiency/accuracy gains are benchmark-specific. [N-HiTS v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12886v6) | Attractive direct multi-horizon baseline with moderate operational complexity. Smooth forecasts may miss abrupt evaluation bursts or billing thresholds; test pooling settings. Current library implementation accepts more covariates than “history-only MLP” shorthand suggests; see version audit below. |
| **TFT** | Explicit static, historical-observed, and known-future inputs; recurrent processing, variable-selection gates and attention; direct quantile outputs trained by quantile loss. [TFT v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.09363v3) | Natural comparator for staffing, calendar and approved-plan features with honest availability timestamps. More moving parts and tuning than a dense model. Marginal horizon quantiles alone do not define a coherent joint path distribution. Variable-selection and attention summaries describe fitted associations, not causal spending effects. |
| **TCN** | Causal, dilated convolutions with residual connections provide a finite receptive field and parallel processing across observed positions. The foundational evaluation compares generic convolutional and recurrent sequence models, rather than enterprise-spend forecasts. [TCN v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01271v2) | Useful small sequence baseline for local patterns or high-frequency activity. Explicitly size receptive field for weekly cycles and task durations. Known-future features need a suitable forecast decoder; “causal convolution” does not by itself establish feature-availability correctness. Point, quantile or likelihood heads are design choices. |
| **DLinear / NLinear** | Very simple linear mappings are serious counterevidence to the assumption that more complex Transformers necessarily forecast better. DLinear separates moving-average trend/remainder before linear prediction; the paper compares nine long-horizon datasets. [LTSF-Linear v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504v3) | Low-cost capacity baseline for stable calendar or level behavior. Original comparisons do not prove universal Transformer failure. Limited nonlinear response and no inherent uncertainty or comprehensive exogenous interface. It is particularly valuable as a check on whether neural tuning adds useful complexity. |
| **PatchTST** | Patches reduce sequence-token count; channels share embedding/Transformer weights but are processed independently. Includes supervised forecasting and masked representation learning. [PatchTST v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.14730v2) | Test for long informative histories and channel-specific regularity. Shared parameters do not imply feature interaction; a launch feature in another channel cannot automatically influence spend. Memory benefits depend on patch/stride configuration. A point-output implementation requires explicit uncertainty construction. |
| **iTransformer** | Embeds each variable's lookback sequence as a token and attends across variables; temporal processing is in feedforward representations. [iTransformer v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06625v4) | Useful if provider/model/team signals carry stable cross-variable information. Sensitive to aligned panel definitions and changing channel composition. Cross-variable attention is not a learned probability of a project happening. Future-covariate support is implementation/version dependent, as the audit demonstrates. |
| **TSMixer / TSMixer-Ext** | Alternates MLP mixing across time and features. The extended variant incorporates static and future auxiliary inputs. The paper finds limited cross-variable benefit on familiar long-horizon datasets but substantial benefit in its M5 retail setup. [TSMixer v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.06053v5) | Particularly relevant counterexample to treating channel independence as universally preferable. Test against time-only mixing and tabular lag models to establish actual value from staffing/project features. Mixer is not synonymous with the extended covariate-aware architecture. |
| **TiDE** | Dense encoder/decoder supports covariates and nonlinear dependencies; a residual path preserves a simpler temporal mapping. Original long-horizon training uses MSE; benchmark speed claims depend on compared configurations. [TiDE v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.08424v5) | A useful lower-complexity covariate comparator to TFT. Dense layers still need tuning, and flattening a long context can raise parameter count. Paper theory for a linear analogue under assumptions is not evidence of enterprise-spend accuracy. |
| **TimeMixer** | Decomposes and mixes multiple temporal scales with past-mixing and multiple future predictors. [TimeMixer v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14616v1) | A targeted test if daily/weekly patterns and bursts coexist. More aggregation choices introduce aliasing and smoothing risks. Do not infer future-context support or a probabilistic joint law from multiscale processing. |
| **TimeXer** | Distinguishes target/endogenous variables from external variables, combining patch self-attention with variable cross-attention. The paper trains squared-error forecasts and evaluates exogenous-variable settings. [TimeXer v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19072v4) | Worth testing when heterogeneous external series are informative. Exogenous does not mean known in the future: feature cutoffs and actual decoder capability must be checked. The paper's causal language is not an identification result for a finance intervention. |

## Inference: target and data constraints dominate architecture

Use a precise modeling target: for example daily **incurred variable usage** or **provider-reported variable charges** within a declared source coverage, currency and attribution basis. Predicting invoice-posting spikes is a different problem from predicting when work occurred. An observation mask and reporting-delay features cannot magically recover an unobserved bill. Keep late-incurred nowcasts separate from future continuation; the final forecast contract should expose both when needed.

A calendar feature is known ahead. Future headcount is known only to the extent a dated plan specifies it; an eventual realized headcount is unavailable at an earlier forecast origin. A newly approved evaluation campaign may be a scenario rather than an unconditional covariate. Store plan version, approval/cancellation state, expected effective window and extraction provenance. Extraction confidence is neither campaign-execution probability nor a spend quantile.

A single model across provider tokens, USD charges and internal credits is meaningless without explicit target/scale separation. Different token types and price schedules matter even when units share the word token. Model variable usage and price/contract effects separately when evidence supports it; otherwise target reported charges directly with its stated limitations. Known fixed fees and included usage should be accounted for outside a learned variable-use forecast to prevent double-counting.

Granularity is an experiment. Company totals are stable but offer few training series. Team × workload/provider series increase the panel while retaining interpretable meaning; excessive splitting creates sparse and changing channels. Individuals can contribute authorized modeling data without individual product views. Transfer of a person, shared credential or project changes series meaning; an ID embedding is not a durable behavioral identity. Pooling unrelated workloads can create negative transfer. Compare global, workload-group and local fits where history permits; never pool across customer boundaries merely for convenience.

There is no defensible universal minimum of days or series for these models. Sliding windows overlap: thousands of windows from one brief rollout are not thousands of independent projects. Learning curves should vary history length and panel size, with untouched periods and teams, and count distinct cycles/events. Small data favors aggressive regularization and simpler candidates until contrary evidence appears. Missing records and valid zero use need distinct encoding, especially when new projects are padded to fit a neural window.

## Established mathematics and proposed uncertainty design

Squared-error prediction targets a conditional mean; absolute-error prediction targets a median, and quantile loss targets its specified quantile under usual population-risk conditions. Architecture names do not determine which was trained. The inspected library defaults for NHITS, TiDE, PatchTST and iTransformer use MAE, so calling their default point outputs “expected spend” would be unjustified. A mean on log-transformed spend is also not a mean in dollars after exponentiation.

**Proposed design:** For cumulative 7/14/30-day and calendar-month-remainder decisions, train/evaluate the desired aggregate directly or aggregate coherent simulated paths before calculating quantiles. Means add; marginal medians and quantiles generally do not. DeepAR offers temporal paths, but summing independently sampled team paths additionally assumes cross-team dependence away. Shared project launches, outages and price changes make that assumption testable. A deterministic multivariate network does not solve this uncertainty problem merely by attending across teams.

Direct aggregate quantile heads can avoid temporal quantile addition, but separately learned 7/14/30-day heads may cross or violate cumulative ordering. Monotonic constraints are appropriate for nonnegative usage, not necessarily signed net charges with refunds. Calendar-month remainder has variable length; use explicit date masks or a horizon design that represents those lengths. Calibration, support, aggregate consistency and uncertainty after a regime change remain separate validations.

## Proposed experiment and deployment decision

Use the same as-of snapshots, ownership mapping and rolling origins for all candidates. Preserve a final untouched period and a held-out-team/cold-start assessment. Fit scalers, vocabularies, imputation and feature selection only on training data. Validation windows used for stopping/tuning cannot serve as the final accuracy result. Compare both matched feature sets and each model's honest full-feature configuration; otherwise an architecture comparison can secretly be a data-access comparison.

Report cumulative bias, absolute error, upper-tail quantile loss, interval coverage/sharpness and budget-exceedance calibration alongside runtime and failure rate. Break out stable usage, intermittent work, new teams, known campaigns, cancellations, provider/model migrations and reporting delays. Include company totals and small teams; scale-normalized global loss can hide finance-material errors, whereas raw-dollar weighting can ignore every small team. Predeclare both views.

Measure training time, peak RAM/VRAM, CPU serving latency, artifact size and refresh cost on the intended hardware. Direct MLPs have a plausible serving-cost advantage; DeepAR pays for recursive sample generation; attention costs depend on patch or variable counts. These are structural expectations, not measured product costs. Tune under comparable budgets, repeat selected configurations across seeds, and separate architecture gains from normalization, lookback and covariate gains. Test history-only, calendar-only and context-augmented variants with sham/shuffled context controls.

Serve trained immutable artifacts behind a forecast job boundary rather than retraining inside the finance assistant. Persist cutoff, target, feature snapshot, code/package version, loss, horizon, calibration state and fallback reason. A failed/OOD model should leave measured spending available and use a validated baseline or an unavailable result. Attention plots and trend decompositions can aid diagnosis; user explanations should cite actual inputs and measured scenario differences without converting associations into asserted causes.

**Unvalidated hypotheses:** (1) pooling related workload series will improve sparse-team forecasts; (2) reliable staffing/project covariates add value beyond recent usage; (3) a dense model can match attention models at lower operating cost; (4) probabilistic paths improve cumulative risk decisions; and (5) any advantage survives price changes, team reorganization and late reporting. Reject each if held-out evidence fails to support it. A neural model's promotion requires repeatable material improvement over simpler alternatives under the product's decision metrics, not a synthetic demo win.
