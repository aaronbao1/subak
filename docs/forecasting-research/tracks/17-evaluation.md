# 17 — Forecast evaluation, selection, and capability promotion

Research date: 2026-10-24. This is a proposed protocol, not an executed benchmark. No representative enterprise billing/context panel has been supplied. The Northstar demonstration, local session sample, and public serving traces cannot establish enterprise forecast accuracy.

## Requirements and evidence boundary

The master plan requires useful company/team planning, separate usage/estimated cost/reported-charge meanings, citations, explicit unavailable states, and internal allocations without provider enforcement. The original internal discussion additionally proposes historical manager-bias assessment and frequent rolling updates. Its dialogue is a requirements source, not empirical evidence that these functions work. See `docs/ai-spend-master-plan.md` §§1–3, backend acceptance checks, `docs/factory-agent-plan.md` forecasting responsibilities, and `docs/forecasting-research/transcripts/internal-team-discussion.txt`.

**Established evidence.** Rolling-origin evaluation fits using preceding observations and can evaluate the intended multistep horizon; training residuals are not forecast errors. [Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, FPP3, §5.10](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html#time-series-cross-validation) [17-S01]. Chronology is a design choice with assumptions: an empirical comparison found blocked cross-validation useful for stationary series, while repeated out-of-sample evaluation worked best for their nonstationary cases. That does not prove one split is best for all processes. [Cerqueira, Torgo and Mozetič, 2020](https://web.cs.dal.ca/~ltorgo/publication/2020_ctm20/) [17-S02]. Finite-sample model-selection scores can themselves be overfit, with biases as large as between-algorithm differences. [Cawley and Talbot, 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) [17-S03]. Repeated adaptive holdout reuse is a separate source of overfitting; formal reusable-holdout mechanisms require assumptions and are not permission to repeatedly inspect an ordinary test set. [Dwork et al., 2015, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629v2) [17-S04].

**Inference.** Adoption changes, late reporting, company-specific contracts and mutable plans make realistic chronological replay the primary enterprise test. A shuffled row split answers the wrong deployment question even if it looks statistically precise. Successful public-benchmark results justify trying a method, not promoting it for this product.

## Immutable forecast-origin and outcome contracts

**Proposed design.** Every scored forecast is keyed by tenant, scope, origin timestamp, target interval, target kind, unit/currency basis, coverage set, source versions, model/configuration version, and forecast issue timestamp. A scheduled origin has a deadline; late output is recorded as late/unavailable for the operational score even when its numeric accuracy is retained diagnostically. Scheduled origins cannot disappear from a model's denominator.

Construct the input snapshot using when each fact became available to this system, not only its event date. Store service/incurrence time, source publication time when known, ingestion time, revision/version identity, validity interval, and authorization scope. Choose the exact revision visible at the origin. Preserve contemporary team maps, contract/price versions, provider coverage, budget version, document content and approved-versus-draft state. A retrospectively dated document first ingested later was not available earlier. Re-running today's extractor on historically available documents is a separate retrospective-method test; it is not a replay of a historical deployed extractor. Store extractor/model versions and input hashes to distinguish these claims. ALFRED provides a concrete primary-source precedent for distinguishing observation dates from vintages and later revisions; it does not establish billing latency. [St. Louis Fed, ALFRED download documentation](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/help/downloaddata) [17-S05].

Define separate labels for physical usage, price-normalized usage cost, estimated charges under a specified price schedule, provider-reported incurred charges, late-incurred nowcasts, future continuation, fixed contractual charges, and the supported reconciled total. A forecast of net charges can include credits or refunds; nonnegative variable usage cannot be forced to share that constraint. Do not compare a usage-only forecast with an all-in invoice. Preserve the native unit, service period and posting period. Score team targets under the declared attribution convention; score company totals separately so reorganization errors cannot hide in cancellation.

For origin `o`, cumulative future targets use a declared half-open interval `[o, o+H)` with `H` in {7,14,30} days; calendar-month remainder ends at the next month boundary in the tenant's declared reporting timezone. These are initial experiment candidates, not validated optimal horizons. A full-period projection additionally combines observed-to-date, late-incurred nowcast and future components without overlapping intervals. Days remaining is a scoring stratum, since a one-day remainder is not comparable to a nearly full month.

The label store retains first release, subsequent revisions, a mature adjudicated label and its availability date. Maturity requires an observed source close/finalization state or a predeclared, source-specific settlement policy estimated only from development vintages. A calendar lag alone cannot establish correctness. Report residual revision size after that policy. Score primary accuracy against the fixed mature label version and separately measure sensitivity to first-release and later revised outcomes. Unresolved labels remain pending, not zero and not silently dropped. Report their counts and properties: high spend may settle later, so complete-case scoring can be biased.

## Chronological selection and generalization

**Proposed design.** Register the target contract, origins, model registry, search budget, split manifest, primary scores and promotion criteria before opening final labels. Define three chronological segments: development `D`, calibration `C`, and final evaluation `F`, with calendar boundaries chosen after inventorying available history and before examining final performance. Do not invent universal percentages or a minimum number of days.

Within `D`, use multiple expanding- and rolling-window origin folds. Fit preprocessing, imputation, scalers, feature selection, covariate forecasts, pooling parameters, reconciliation, hyperparameters and ensemble weights using only permitted earlier data. For a training row with origin `s`, its complete target must have ended and its label must have become available by the fit cutoff. Purge supervised examples that would cross a validation origin or depend on unavailable outcomes. Choose the gap from horizon and actual availability rules, not an arbitrary embargo. Later portions of the same usage burst may not leak through another aggregation.

Choose the numerical recipe and routing policy in `D`; fit interval/breach calibration in `C`; assess once in `F`. If calibration rules or thresholds are selected among alternatives, nest that selection within earlier chronological calibration folds or use an additional untouched calibration segment. Refitting after calibration can change residual distributions, so validate the complete refit-plus-calibration sequence. A registered prequential policy may learn from labels that mature during `F`, as deployment would, but may not use final performance reports to redesign itself. Retain a frozen-policy comparison. Any final-informed redesign requires a new future final window; label it development if reused.

Cross these temporal splits with distinct population tasks:

- **Warm team:** future origins for teams observed in training.
- **Unseen team, known company:** remove that team's series, labels, learned identifiers and derivatives from fitting; allow only company context and team information actually available at deployment.
- **Unseen company:** hold out the entire tenant, including document-derived embeddings, mappings, fitted preprocessing and calibration outcomes. Global training still obeys the forecast origin; another company's later history is future information too.
- **Short-history adaptation:** evaluate the same unseen population after its first available observations, with adaptation prefixes fixed in the manifest. Distinguish actual team/company formation from artificial history hiding.

Holding out projects/users from their parent team is not independent company evidence. Keep shared accounts, duplicated exports, rebranded subsidiaries and common underlying workloads in the same split group. Evaluate pooled methods only where tenant permissions permit pooling. If there is one company, cross-company performance is unmeasured regardless of the number of users or daily rows.

## Metrics, dependence, and sufficient support

**Established evidence.** Proper scores reward honest distributions under their scoring assumptions, rather than narrow intervals alone. [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) [17-S06]. WIS evaluates quantile/central-interval forecasts with width and miss penalties; CRPS requires an appropriate distribution representation. The WIS article has a 2022 correction to example values/figures, which should accompany implementation references. [Bracher et al., 2021](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008618), [correction](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010592) [17-S07]. Equal-predictive-accuracy tests can account for serially correlated loss differentials, but their assumptions and finite-sample behavior matter. [Diebold and Mariano, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599) [17-S08].

**Proposed design.** Report MAE, RMSE and signed bias with a fixed sign convention `prediction − actual`; use the predicted median for MAE and mean for squared-error evaluation where available. MAE communicates typical absolute money/unit error; RMSE emphasizes large misses; bias detects persistent over/understatement. Do not label a median as an expected-spend estimate. Report dollar totals, equal-team macro averages and equal-company macro averages; prespecify any spend weighting and use origin-known weights. MASE uses a training-only denominator and is unavailable for constant/insufficient histories; MAPE is inappropriate around zeros. Do not manufacture epsilon denominators to conceal undefined scores.

Use pinball loss at registered quantiles, WIS on one common quantile grid, or CRPS on distribution/sample outputs. Report interval width and empirical coverage together, by horizon, maturity/coverage class, spend scale and regime when supported. Score breach probabilities with Brier score and reliability plots against the same fixed threshold event. For joint paths or team aggregates, score each required cumulative/aggregate target and inspect dependence-sensitive path behavior; correct daily marginals do not validate monthly uncertainty. Quantiles must not be added to produce aggregate quantiles.

Daily 30-day targets overlap heavily; three horizons share outcomes; teams share company shocks. Report raw forecast count, distinct companies/teams, distinct settled periods, breach episodes and non-overlapping horizon blocks. Pair model losses on identical cases. Proposed uncertainty analysis resamples companies and contiguous time blocks while keeping all teams/horizons together inside blocks; block length is justified from overlap and residual dependence with sensitivity analysis. With too few companies, report within-company uncertainty and per-company results, not a fictitious population confidence interval. A Diebold–Mariano test is optional supporting evidence, not a substitute for deployment-sized effect estimates or a license for many uncorrected comparisons. Predeclare primary contrasts; treat post-hoc slice discoveries as hypotheses.

There is no defensible universal “enough data” threshold here. Before promotion, provide a support table for each claimed capability: independent population units, time coverage, settled target count, effective dependence, observed regime diversity and event counts. Set required precision from a finance-approved decision tolerance and a pilot-based dependence-aware power/precision analysis, using development data only. If there are no meaningful breach episodes, warning recall and lead-time quality are unknown. If a 30-day window plus settlement cannot mature before scoring, the label is unavailable. More rolling rows do not repair those deficiencies.

## Manager forecasts, selection agents, and decision utility

**Established evidence.** A study of more than 60,000 supply-chain forecasts found average judgmental improvement in three of four companies, while small and upward adjustments often harmed accuracy. This is evidence to measure contextual judgment, not a transferable rule that managers overestimate AI spending. [Fildes et al., 2009](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/45487/) [17-S09]. Agent-evaluation research identifies insufficient holdouts, cost-blind accuracy comparisons and weak reproducibility. [Kapoor et al., 2024 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502v1) [17-S10]. GIFT-Eval supplies broad forecasting tasks and explicit pretraining/test separation, not enterprise billing/context validation. [Aksu et al., 2024 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10393v2) [17-S11].

**Proposed design.** Archive manager forecasts at the original timestamp with target, point functional, rationale, uncertainty if elicited, and information access. A budget request or stretch target is not necessarily a predictive mean. Compare manager/model forecasts at equal deadlines; report both matched subsets and missing-submission rates. Estimate any manager/team bias correction only from earlier settled matched pairs, shrink weak individual evidence toward a pooled prior, and validate against uncorrected forecasts. A manager may only submit difficult cases, and incentives or roles can change; retrospective bias must not become a personal capability claim. Manager forecasts can improve a blend even if individually less accurate, which E10 must test.

Evaluate an adaptive selector as one complete algorithm: input inspection, candidates proposed, trials run, failure handling, model chosen, calibration and delivered prediction. Freeze its prompt, tools, permitted registry and data access. Record all trials, seeds, tokens, inference/training dollars, wall time, human interventions and cache policy. Budget-match deterministic/random search and a fixed simple ensemble; report accuracy-cost frontiers rather than only the best unlimited run. An oracle that picks the lowest final loss is a diagnostic ceiling, never an operational comparator. Fit ensemble weights on out-of-fold development forecasts; distinguish a mixture of distributions from quantile averaging, then evaluate the chosen combination itself. Do not hide failed runs or pick the best agent seed after viewing final outcomes.

Record each foundation model's exact artifact/API revision, declared pretraining corpus/cutoff where available, benchmark overlap audit and adaptation mode. Unknown pretraining provenance stays unknown. Public leaderboard performance, repeated test feedback, known future documents and cross-tenant duplicates are separate contamination routes. Prefer private prospective outcomes for final claims; public benchmarks remain useful for implementation checks and comparison breadth. Licenses of papers, code, datasets and model weights require separate verification.

Operational warning evaluation fixes the budget version known at each origin. Define an event precisely, for example mature full-period incurred charges exceeding that origin's allocation. A later increase must not erase the original event label. Score episode-level first actionable warning lead time, missed-event rate, false alerts per eligible team-period, duplicate/retracted alerts, and review minutes; show precision/recall and event prevalence. Choose persistence/cooldown and alert thresholds in development from finance's costs and required lead time. Never report lead time only among detected events without reporting misses.

Evaluate abstention with availability versus conditional error curves, explicit reason codes, time-to-recovery, fallback quality and total service loss including review/delay cost. Compare models on both shared-output cases and all scheduled cases with the registered fallback. Abstention costs and asymmetric under/overforecast costs require stakeholder elicitation; report sensitivity curves until values exist. Allocation decision simulations must preserve envelope arithmetic, priorities and approval authority. An internal budget transfer does not mechanically reduce provider spending: retrospective allocation regret is scenario evidence, and causal savings require an intervention study or credible causal design.

## Promotion by capability and open hypotheses

**Proposed design.** Gate each scope/target/horizon independently. (1) Replay/accounting integrity allows a clearly labeled demonstration, not accuracy claims. (2) Point forecasts require mature labels, adequate support, acceptable bias/availability and a final paired improvement or justified cost/value tradeoff against baselines. (3) Intervals require proper-score and calibration evidence on their actual cumulative target; point accuracy alone is insufficient. (4) Breach warnings additionally require episode support, lead time and acceptable false-alert/review costs. (5) Numerical context adjustment requires paired incremental value beyond history-only and robustness to irrelevant, stale or conflicting context; otherwise context remains explanatory/scenario-only. (6) Cold-start/pooling and automated model selection require their own held-out-population and whole-procedure evidence. (7) Decision recommendations require constraint correctness and prospective decision-use evidence, without claiming causal savings from prediction accuracy.

Finance sets material error tolerances, false-alert/review costs and useful lead times before final evaluation. Research does not invent pass percentages. Promotion records retain the exact validated population, supported target basis and remaining source gaps. A model can be promoted for warm-team 7-day usage while abstaining for new-company monthly charges. Monitoring repeats the same maturity-aware scorecard; distribution shifts or insufficient recent support can narrow capabilities rather than silently preserving a universal “validated” label.

**Unvalidated hypotheses.** Context may improve change episodes while harming ordinary periods; pooling may reduce cold-start error while transferring contract-specific bias; an ensemble may outperform a model selector at lower operational cost; recent-window models may adapt faster but lose seasonal information. E03/E06/E09/E11 plus regime-stratified final replay can overturn each hypothesis. No proposed design here establishes which family wins or how large enterprise performance gains will be.
